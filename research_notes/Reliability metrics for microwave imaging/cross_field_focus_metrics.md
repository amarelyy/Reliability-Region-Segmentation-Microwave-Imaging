# Ground-truth-free focus / coherence / consistency quality indices from neighbouring fields, for beamformed microwave breast imaging

Method note: about 20 search/fetch calls. Many primary full texts were blocked (PMC bot check, MDPI 403, a university repository DNS failure, a PDF returned as binary). Most findings below are from search-result summaries or abstracts, and are tagged UNVERIFIED where I did not see the primary text. Formulas marked "standard (my write-up)" are textbook definitions that I wrote out in NumPy-ready form; I did not confirm them in a fetched source unless a source link is given. Earlier notes in `RRS MBI reliability score fixes/` already cover: CF definition and Mallart-Fink/Li-Li/Camacho citations, Elahi 2018 (CF-DAS misplaces peaks), Reimer 2020 metrics, selective-classification theory (AUROC, AURC, risk-coverage, cluster bootstrap, Holm), and the generic "agreement across views estimates variance" argument. They are not repeated here. This note adds primary-field metrics and the cross-field failure evidence.

## Q1. Coherence-based quality indices (CF, GCF, PCF/SCF, SLSC, lag-one coherence) as per-pixel or per-image reliability measures; evidence they track positional accuracy

### Takeaway
In ultrasound, coherence of channel data is validated as a proxy for image quality (contrast, CNR, SNR of channels, phase aberration), not for positional accuracy of a peak. In through-wall radar, CF has been proposed as an autofocus metric, because wrong propagation parameters degrade it. I found no source that shows CF or SLSC predicts localization error of a detected target, and the microwave-breast literature warns that CF can reward non-lesion responses.

### Cited Findings
- Lag-one coherence (LOC), Long, Bottenus, Trahey, IEEE TUFFC 2018: LOC is normalized spatial coherence between neighbouring channels at lag m = 1, averaged over element pairs inside an axial kernel/ROI. With additive uncorrelated channel noise, noise-free coherence at lag m != 0 is scaled by SNR/(1+SNR); for a coherent point target (large aperture) LOC is approx SNR/(1+SNR), so channel SNR is approx LOC/(1-LOC); speckle: LOC = [SNR/(1+SNR)](1-1/M). In vivo, LOC correlated with contrast (r = 0.74) and CNR (r = 0.66). Stated limitations: assumes triangular noise-free coherence and uncorrelated noise, "do not fully hold in vivo"; variability grows below about -10 dB channel SNR because single-channel signals lack summation gain. — [PMC6378881](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6378881) (fetched, read)
- LOC is used mostly as an ROI value at the focus for real-time feedback to adapt imaging parameters (Bottenus 2018, Long 2018), and as a clutter estimator (Long 2020, LoSCAN). In liver imaging (Offerdahl 2022) LOC revealed regions of suppressed coherence that look like uniform speckle on B-mode. No standard LOC threshold exists. — [search summary of Duke records](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1369065) (UNVERIFIED beyond the summary)
- SLSC (Lediju et al.): SLSC images have higher SNR and CNR than matched B-mode in speckle phantoms but limited point-target conspicuity. A simulation study found SLSC and harmonic SLSC correlate with in-situ pressure-related phase-aberration measures (R^2 0.67 and 0.77). Cardiologists rated endocardial border visibility 22-33 % better with SLSC when B-mode quality was poor. — [PMC3172134 (could not be opened; summary via search)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3172134), [PMC4821635](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4821635) (both UNVERIFIED, snippet only)
- Reverberation clutter decorrelates quickly at very short lags, which is the stated mechanism behind SLSC clutter suppression — same search summaries (UNVERIFIED).
- Through-wall radar: Burkholder and Browne, "Coherence Factor Enhancement of Through-Wall Radar Images", IEEE AWPL 9:842-845, 2010. CF = ratio of coherent to incoherent power at each image point; they found that through-wall propagation degrades CF and that CF "can serve as a quantitative metric for autofocus and image correction". Whether the paper actually runs such an autofocus is not confirmed. — [abstract record](https://search.isc.ac/inventory/49/1302103.htm) (UNVERIFIED, abstract only; DOI 10.1109/LAWP.2010.2069078 per the search summary)
- Wall-parameter autofocus in through-wall SAR: Jin, Chen, Zhou, IEEE TGRS 2012, estimate wall thickness and permittivity by tuning until behind-wall targets focus best (image-domain variant avoids re-imaging at each trial). Ahmad and Amin, SPIE 6547, 2007, propose higher-order standardized moments of the image as smearing measures to tune wall variables, and note the autofocus solution is not unique. — [Jin et al. record](https://bohrium.dp.tech/paper/arxiv/556f63a02401b4b38c2363be), [SPIE 6547](https://photonicsforenergy.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/6547/654709/Performance-of-autofocusing-schemes-for-single-target-and-populated-scenes/10.1117/12.720085.full) (UNVERIFIED, abstracts only)
- Forward-looking GPR: coherence-factor-based rough-surface clutter suppression (MDPI Remote Sensing 12(5):857). I could not open the full text; only that CF is applied as an image weight to suppress clutter. — [link](https://mdpi.com/2072-4292/12/5/857) (UNVERIFIED)
- CF used as a per-pixel weight, with sub-aperture size selected as a function of coherence (e.g. 32 of 64 elements), appears in ultrasound patents; high CF is read as better focusing quality. — [US7744532](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7744532) (patent text, UNVERIFIED as peer-reviewed practice)
- Ultrasound adaptive beamformers (MV, EBMV, coherence-based, DMAS) produce "dark region artifacts" next to strong targets, so measured contrast can falsely look higher: the weights misclassify mainlobe vs sidelobe energy. — [Rindal, Rodriguez-Molares, Austeng 2017, NTNU preprint](https://ntnuopen.ntnu.no/ntnu-xmlui/handle/11250/2486897) (abstract level; full PDF fetch failed)

### Inferences
- NumPy forms, standard (my write-up, not read from a source here). Let `s[i, x]` be the delayed (focused) complex channel data for pair or antenna i at pixel x, N channels.
  - CF(x) = |sum_i s_i|^2 / (N * sum_i |s_i|^2). Phase CF: PCF = max(0, 1 - (sigma_phi / sigma_0)); sigma_phi = std of instantaneous phase across channels (use circular std: sqrt(-2 ln |mean(exp(j*phi_i))|)). Sign CF: SCF = sqrt(1 - (std(sign(Re s_i)) ... )) i.e. 1 - sqrt(1 - |mean(b_i)|^2) with b_i = sign(real(s_i)).
  - Lag-one coherence at a pixel: LOC = Re( sum_i s_i conj(s_{i+1}) ) / sqrt( sum_i |s_i|^2 * sum_i |s_{i+1}|^2 ), averaged over a small spatial kernel (here kernel = neighbouring pixels along the delay surface, or neighbouring antenna positions i, i+1 on the 72-position ring). SLSC(x) = sum_{m=1..M} R(m), R(m) = mean over i of the normalized correlation between i and i+m. In the monostatic-like 72-position ring, "lag" = angular step between positions.
  - Inverting LOC to channel SNR (SNR = LOC/(1-LOC)) is a calibratable per-pixel noise-floor estimate with no ground truth.
- None of these is validated against peak position error in the sources found, only against contrast/CNR/SNR. Treat any link to LE as untested for microwave breast data. A fair test would be the same one the project already runs: AUROC for LE <= 15 mm against a "predict the centre" baseline.
- Through-wall CF-as-autofocus is the closest precedent for using CF as a ground-truth-free self-consistency index. It works there because the free parameter (wall) is global and only a single bright target is needed. The analogue here is scanning the breast permittivity/phase-centre/shell-centre and maximizing a coherence metric at the peak, which is a per-scan calibration rather than a reliability score.
- LOC-based channel-SNR is attractive because it is computed from the scan alone, but its derivation assumes a flat noise floor and coherent echoes at the assumed delay; the shell echo that is coherent across channels would also give high LOC, so the project must evaluate LOC on adipose-only references at the same radius (same trap as CF).

### Gaps
- No primary full text read for Lediju 2011 (SLSC), Mallart-Fink, Li-Li, Camacho, Burkholder-Browne. No source found that correlates CF, SLSC or LOC with positional (not contrast) error.
- Exact PCF/SCF formulas from Camacho et al. 2009 not verified here; the forms above are standard but should be checked against the paper before coding.

## Q2. Autofocus / focus-quality metrics (entropy, kurtosis, Gini, sharpness, contrast, PSLR, peak-to-second-peak, resolution-cell area): definitions and validation

### Takeaway
Image-based focus metrics (entropy, sharpness sum I^gamma, normalized std/mean contrast, kurtosis) are validated in SAR/sonar/GPR as objective functions for estimating a global nuisance parameter (phase error, velocity, wall parameters). Several sources show they pick biased answers when the scene has bright clutter or low contrast. No source validates them as per-image confidence scores for localization accuracy.

### Cited Findings
- Sparsity measure definitions (Hurley and Rickard, IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 2009, arXiv 0811.4706). Gini index for coefficients sorted ascending c_(1) <= ... <= c_(N): S = 1 - 2 * sum_k [ c_(k)/||c||_1 * (N - k + 1/2)/N ]. Kurtosis-type measure: sum c^4 / (sum c^2)^2. Shannon entropy: -sum c~ log c~^2 with c~ = c^2/||c||_2^2. Six criteria compared (Robin Hood, Scaling, Rising Tide, Cloning, Bill Gates, Babies); the arXiv abstract says Gini is the only one of 15 measures satisfying all six, while an IEEE metadata record says two measures (pq-mean with p <= 1 < q, and Gini) do. — [ar5iv text](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/0811.4706) (formulas read); conflict noted by [search summary](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0811.4706)
- SAR autofocus: iterative phase-error search that optimizes a sharpness or contrast metric. Fienup, Optics Letters 2000, uses a sharpness metric generalizing the Muller-Buffington squared-intensity metric (sum I^gamma). A sonar (SAS) study defines sum-of-squared-intensity sharpness and a normalized std-over-mean contrast, and reports the sharpness metric gives a function with many local maxima, hard to bound. — [Fienup OL 2000 (listed; fetch failed)](https://labsites.rochester.edu/fienup/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/OL00_SARFocMaxSharp.pdf), [SAS manuscript](https://www.krakenrobotics.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/2018-Manuscript_SASSAR_Myers_Williams_Dillon_revised.pdf.pdf) (both UNVERIFIED, summary only)
- A Sandia analysis found PGA outperforms entropy-minimizing image-metric autofocus in low-contrast SAR scenes. — [Sandia record](https://sandia.gov/research/publications/details/analysis-of-sar-autofocus-performance-2012-10-01) (UNVERIFIED, summary)
- GPR: Xu, Miller, Rappaport pick the migration velocity that gives the best-focused image with an entropy-like criterion (landmine localization). A Vietnamese journal paper says accurate velocity gives minimum entropy or maximum energy in the migrated section. Delf et al. (JGR, Svalbard glacier) use negative entropy to build a spatially varying migration velocity field. GPR diffraction-focusing methods use kurtosis (and local slopes) across constant-velocity migrations, where the attribute has an extremum at the right velocity. — [search summary](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.05308) and [MDPI 2813-477X/3/4/24](https://www.mdpi.com/2813-477x/3/4/24) (UNVERIFIED beyond summary; MDPI fetch returned 403)
- Failure case: a 2026 arXiv paper on GPR imaging of tree-trunk defects found an entropy-based autofocus criterion strongly underestimated effective permittivity (mean relative error 50.3 % vs 4.46 % for an SSIM-based criterion), with entropy favouring over-focused images because it scores overall energy concentration. — [arXiv 2602.05308](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.05308) (from search summary; the PDF itself exceeded the fetch size limit, so numbers UNVERIFIED)
- Through-wall: autofocus solution from moment-based metrics is non-unique (Ahmad and Amin 2007, above).

### Inferences
- NumPy forms, standard (my write-up). Let `I = |image|` or `|image|^2` over a mask M (breast disc, not the whole grid), p = I / sum(I).
  - Entropy H = -sum p log p (lower = better focused). Normalized: H / log(numel).
  - Contrast C = std(I)/mean(I). Sharpness S_gamma = sum I^gamma (gamma around 2).
  - Kurtosis-like K = sum I^4 / (sum I^2)^2 (formula as in Hurley-Rickard above, usable directly). Gini from the sorted formula above (vectorized: sort, then `1 - 2*np.sum((a/a.sum())*(N-k+0.5)/N)`).
  - Peak-to-sidelobe ratio PSLR = I_peak / max(I outside the main-lobe region), where main lobe = connected region above half-power (-3 dB amplitude / -6 dB intensity) or first null; peak-to-second-peak PSP = I_peak / I_second where second peak is the largest local maximum after excluding a disc of radius r_excl around peak (r_excl = FWHM of the point-spread function, computed from a simulated point scatterer by sim.py).
  - Resolution cell area (Rayleigh / half-power): A_hp = number of pixels in the connected component of {I >= 0.5 * I_peak} containing the peak, times pixel area. Compare A_hp with the PSF area at the same radius (also from simulation), giving an "excess blur" ratio A_hp / A_PSF(r).
- Entropy/Gini/kurtosis rank an image as better focused whenever energy is concentrated, regardless of where. A single sharp shell ring or an artifact peak scores as well as a true target, which matches the project's own finding (peak at the focused shell ring). These are global-parameter objective functions, not reliability scores. A defensible use is the SAR-style one: scan the nuisance parameters that are really uncertain in this dataset (shell centre offset, permittivity, radius) and take the one maximizing a metric computed inside the breast disc, then report how much the metric gain depends on the parameter (flat objective = uncertain geometry).
- Minimum-entropy autofocus is reported as less reliable in low-contrast scenes (Sandia) and as over-focusing (GPR 2026). UM-BMID scans are low-contrast. Expect entropy to be dominated by clutter.
- Scores should be computed after normalizing by the same score on adipose-only reference reconstructions at the same radial position. This turns a raw metric into a target-free-scan z-score, which is the main way to cancel central or shell bias (see Q5).

### Gaps
- No source found that validates entropy, kurtosis, Gini, PSLR or peak-to-second-peak against localization error as a per-image confidence in radar, GPR, SAR or ultrasound.
- PSLR/ISLR and peak-to-second-peak as no-ground-truth "ambiguity" measures: searched only implicitly; no citable source retrieved. The definitions above are my own.
- SAR minimum-entropy papers (Sandia, adaptive order) seen only as titles/summaries.

## Q3. Migration/velocity-analysis consistency: semblance, CIG flatness, sub-aperture / sub-band agreement; jackknife or bootstrap over channels

### Takeaway
Seismic imaging has a well-developed family of consistency measures: semblance (coherent-sum energy over input energy across offsets), differential semblance and CIG flatness (agreement across offset/angle subsets), and a published per-location "velocity confidence" attribute built from CIG flatness. I found no jackknife/bootstrap-over-channels method in these fields; the sub-aperture/sub-offset split is the established analogue.

### Cited Findings
- Semblance is the most common coherency measure: a normalized energy ratio between the stacked output trace and the input traces; velocity is picked where semblance peaks. Migrated events should be flat across offset/angle gathers when velocity is correct, and non-flatness indicates velocity error. Differential semblance measures non-flatness by comparing neighbouring bins; for layered-acoustic CMP analysis it is reported to have no secondary minima. — [SEG wiki, Coherency inversion](https://wiki.seg.org/wiki/Coherency_inversion), [Stanford SEP 100](https://sepwww.stanford.edu/data/media/public/docs/sep100/bill2/paper_html/node2.html)
- A 2010 comparison of coherence measures for automatic time-migration velocity analysis judges them by the flatness of the resulting CIGs; it includes conventional semblance, differential semblance and two newer measures based on extended images. — [WIT report 2010, Maciel et al.](https://www.wit.uni-hamburg.de/import/documents/reports/2010/wit2010-maciel.pdf) (summary; UNVERIFIED details)
- Seismic velocity confidence (SVC) attribute (2015 abstract, EAGE/earthdoc): calculated by cross-correlating each trace in a CIG with its lower-offset/angle neighbour, to flag areas of less reliable imaging and amplitudes; CIG flatness consistently correlated with fault shadows in the example. — [earthdoc 2214-4609.201412611](https://www.earthdoc.org/content/papers/10.3997/2214-4609.201412611) (abstract only, UNVERIFIED venue; the search summary called it EAGE not SEG)
- Caveat from the same search: several velocity models can honour the data and give the same overall gather flatness, so flatness alone does not certify correctness; some groups add model-uncertainty attributes on top. — [TGS E&P article (PGS workflow)](https://tgs.com/hubfs/Technical%20Library/Technical%20Library%20Files/ep_bell_etal_june2017_modeluncertainty.pdf) (UNVERIFIED, summary)
- GPR: Liu, Irving, Holliger use diffraction semblance for RMS velocity estimation; Fomel et al. use local kurtosis for diffraction focusing. — [GPR focusing search summary](https://www.mdpi.com/2813-477x/3/4/24) (UNVERIFIED)
- In ultrasound, a Japanese study estimates the phase coherence factor from sub-aperture outputs, which "could further enhance" PCF without suppressing diffuse scatter; a patent uses sub-aperture size chosen by coherence. — [search summary of US7744532 and related](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7744532) (UNVERIFIED)

### Inferences
- Mapping to this project (all my reasoning, not published for microwave imaging):
  - Sub-offset analogue: each antenna position pair has a "range" (tx-rx separation or angular sector). Form K sector images I_k(x) (disjoint angular sectors of the 72 positions, or odd/even positions, or contiguous halves). Semblance across sectors at the peak pixel x*: SEMB(x*) = |sum_k I_k(x*)|^2 / (K * sum_k |I_k(x*)|^2) using complex (pre-envelope) sector images. This is CF computed at a coarser level (sub-aperture) and is less sensitive to single-channel noise.
  - Gather flatness analogue: a "CIG" here is I_k(x) versus sector index k. Residual moveout would appear as the peak shifting with sector angle; fit a plane or shift: displacement d_k = argmax of cross-correlation of I_k with the mean over the others (this is what the SVC attribute does with neighbouring traces). Reliability = spread of {x*_k} (std of per-sector peak positions) and the mean normalized cross-correlation between neighbouring sectors. The project already found half-split and sub-band peak-distance AUROC about 0.59-0.60, which this literature explains as the same idea.
  - Differential semblance: sum_k || I_k - I_{k+1} ||^2 over a window around x*, normalized by sum_k || I_k ||^2.
  - Leave-one-out (jackknife) over positions: drop block b of 72/B positions (B = 6-12), recompute only the weighted sum for the pixels (add and subtract, cheap because the image is linear in channels), take the peak each time, and use jackknife variance of x* = ((B-1)/B) * sum_b |x*_(b) - mean|^2. This is standard jackknife statistics (my write-up); no radar/GPR/seismic paper found that does it, so UNVERIFIED as precedent.
  - Sub-band: same with frequency blocks (1-8 GHz split into e.g. 3-4 bands). A systematic geometry error (radius, shell centre) moves the peak differently in different bands because phase error scales with frequency, so sub-band inconsistency detects wrong geometry but sub-band agreement does not detect a common bias such as shell echo (agreement cap noted in earlier notes).
- Flatness/semblance can be high for a coherent artifact (shell ring), as the TGS note says flatness does not guarantee correctness. Always compare against the adipose-reference distribution.

### Gaps
- No jackknife/bootstrap-over-channels reliability score found in radar, GPR, ultrasound or seismic papers I reached. May exist in ultrasound localization-microscopy uncertainty or in DOA estimation; not searched.
- SEG wiki and SEP pages fetched only as summaries; formulas for semblance beyond the verbal definition not verified.
- SVC attribute: full text not read; exact windowing not known.

## Q4. Multi-beamformer agreement (DAS vs MVDR/Capon vs DMAS) as an uncertainty cue

### Takeaway
I found no paper that uses disagreement between beamformers as a per-pixel or per-image confidence. Beamformers are compared on resolution, sidelobes and contrast. Documented failure modes of adaptive beamformers (dark-region artifacts, signal cancellation) mean that disagreement can reflect artefacts of the adaptive method rather than ground-truth uncertainty.

### Cited Findings
- Photoacoustic: minimum-variance-based DMAS (MVB-DMAS) and eigenspace MV + DMAS were developed to outperform DAS, DMAS and MV in resolution and sidelobes; EIBMV-DMAS improved SNR by about 75 % and 34 % relative to DMAS and EIBMV in simulation. DAS is described as a blind beamformer with wide mainlobe and high sidelobes. — [arXiv 1709.06523](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.06523), [arXiv 1709.07965](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.07965v3) (search-summary level)
- The search for "DAS-vs-MV disagreement uncertainty map" returned nothing relevant. — [search result list above](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.07405) (negative result, listed items were method papers)
- Dark region artifact in MV/EBMV/coherence-based/DMAS beamformers: weights treat some mainlobe energy as sidelobe, producing falsely dark areas and inflated contrast. — [Rindal et al. 2017](https://ntnuopen.ntnu.no/ntnu-xmlui/handle/11250/2486897) (abstract level)
- In breast microwave imaging, in the patient data of Elahi et al. 2018, DMAS gave the best images and correct detection in all 5 patients, while DAS was missed or misplaced in one and CF-DAS missed 2 of 5; both CR-DAS and RCB often enhanced residual clutter from preprocessing. — [Elahi 2018 PMC6022049](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6022049/) (already in earlier notes)

### Inferences
- Candidate cue (my construction, untested): per scan, compute peaks x*_DAS, x*_DMAS, x*_CF-DAS (and optionally Capon with diagonal loading, which is implementable from the 72-channel snapshot covariance only with heavy loading since N = 72 channels but few time/frequency snapshots; use frequency sub-bands as snapshots, with forward-backward averaging). Disagreement D_bf = max pairwise distance, or an image-level correlation between normalized maps (Pearson r of the maps inside the breast disc).
- All of DAS, DMAS and CF-DAS use the same data and delays, so they share bias (shell echo, geometry error); disagreement can only expose variance-like differences. Expected to behave like the sub-aperture cue: weak signal, capped by common bias. Testing is cheap because DMAS and CF are already in the pipeline.
- Capon/MVDR needs a covariance estimate; with frequency-domain data of 1001 points one can form the snapshot from sub-band pieces. The dark-region artifact and signal cancellation under steering-vector (geometry) error are the well-known risk, and the project has a geometry error of its own (shell centre offset about 13-15 mm), so Capon is likely to cancel the true target. Do not use Capon as a reference without robust loading. UNVERIFIED reasoning.

### Gaps
- No literature found that evaluates beamformer disagreement against localization error in any field. Not found in microwave breast imaging either.

## Q5. Reference-based clutter/noise floor and change-detection statistics as detection confidence

### Takeaway
The closest published ground-truth-free detection statistics in microwave breast imaging are a Max/Avg image ratio for classifying healthy vs non-healthy images and contralateral comparisons; a differential-imaging thesis exists. None is validated as a localization-confidence score. The target-free-scan null distribution idea is standard statistics and fits the data (adipose-only references), but I found no microwave paper that uses a per-radius null.

### Cited Findings
- A 2018 ESR poster used a Max/Avg parameter (image maximum over image average): healthy breasts averaged about 1.64, non-healthy about 10 % higher, sensitivity 91 % with a threshold T. — [ESR poster C-1390](https://epos.myesr.org/poster/esr/ecr2018/C-1390/Results) (poster, snippet-level; a clinical system, not UM-BMID)
- A 2025 transmission-based study found the cancerous-to-contralateral breast average-permittivity ratio differs significantly from ratios in healthy volunteers (CC view). — [PMC12431958](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12431958/) (snippet)
- A University of Manitoba master's thesis covers differential imaging with time-shift alignment and skin suppression (boundary-aware beamforming); statistic not retrieved. — [mspace](https://mspace.lib.umanitoba.ca/bitstream/handle/1993/35228/rana_muhammadmasud.pdf) (table of contents only)
- Reimer 2020: ideal skin suppression subtracts an adipose-only scan from the full scan; residual mismatch still leaves clutter, worse in DAS than DMAS. — [PMC7345629](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7345629/) (already in earlier notes)
- Elahi 2018: false-positive rejection (left-right breast comparison, classification by magnitude/shape) proposed as future work. — [PMC6022049](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6022049/)
- Through-wall radar: entropy-based wall-clutter rejection has been proposed to remove front-wall returns while sparing target fields. — [search summary](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/AD1003614.pdf) (UNVERIFIED)

### Inferences
- Reference-null recipe (my write-up, standard statistics, UNVERIFIED as published for UM-BMID): reconstruct all adipose-only reference scans (141 in the project's data) with the same pipeline, giving null images N_j(x). For each location bin (radial annulus r/R, maybe also azimuth), estimate mean mu(r) and std sigma(r) of: image amplitude, CF, entropy, SLSC/LOC, sub-aperture spread. For a test scan with peak at x*: z_amp = (I(x*) - mu_I(r*)) / sigma_I(r*); likewise z_CF; or an empirical p-value p = fraction of null-image maxima within the annulus that exceed I(x*). The project already found CF within 10 mm of centre is about 3x the breast mean on adipose-only scans, so a radius-conditioned null is required.
- Max-over-image null (extreme-value style, standard multiple-testing idea): the detection statistic should be compared with the distribution of the image maximum of null scans (max over all pixels), not the pixel value at a fixed place. Empirical quantile of the null maximum gives a family-wise p-value; coded as `np.mean(null_max >= obs_max)`. This corresponds to the Max/Avg ratio of the poster above, but made proper by using reference scans.
- Differential statistic with the phantom's own reference: use `adi_ref_id`-paired reference and `fib_ref_id` where available (the project found fib_ref_id usable for 1/504 scans), so only the adipose reference works. Compute T = ||I_scan - I_ref||_2 inside the breast disc, normalized by the spread of T across adipose-adipose reference pairs (reference-to-reference variability = noise floor). SNR-like detection index: peak(I_scan - I_ref) / sd(I_ref_i - I_ref_j) over pixel set. All computable from scan and reference only.
- Interleaved or neighbouring-position difference (noise floor from repeated or adjacent positions) is another no-ground-truth noise floor, but the project found the interleave measure near zero for 90 % of scans, so skip it.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed microwave breast detection-confidence statistic validated against localization error found.
- Differential imaging thesis details, Max/Avg poster details, and the 2025 permittivity-ratio paper only seen as snippets.
- Did not search for CFAR (constant false alarm rate) detectors in radar, which is the main radar precedent for a reference-noise-based detection threshold. Recommended next search: "CFAR detector clutter map radar" and "clutter map".

## Q6. Known failure modes of these indices (bright clutter, central bias, near-field geometry)

### Takeaway
There is direct published evidence that coherence and sharpness metrics are rewarded by clutter and over-focusing: microwave-breast CF-DAS and IDAS misplaced the dominant response despite suppressing clutter; entropy over-focuses and under-estimates permittivity in GPR; adaptive beamformers create dark-region artifacts that inflate contrast; moment-based autofocus is non-unique; velocity-flatness is not unique in seismic. I found no source explicitly naming a central bias of coherence indices; the central bias argument stays an inference.

### Cited Findings
- Microwave breast: coherence metrics "may reward responses from non-lesion regions within the breast"; coherence-based algorithms suppressed clutter but misplaced the dominant response; IDAS and CF-DAS missed or mislocalised lesions in several cases. — [Elahi 2018 PMC6022049](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6022049/)
- Low-contrast SAR scenes defeat entropy-based autofocus relative to PGA. — [Sandia](https://sandia.gov/research/publications/details/analysis-of-sar-autofocus-performance-2012-10-01) (summary)
- GPR entropy criterion favours over-focused images (MRE 50.3 %). — [arXiv 2602.05308](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.05308) (summary)

### Inferences
- Near-field/geometry: all coherence and focus indices are evaluated at assumed delays, so any common geometry error (radius, shell-centre offset, permittivity) is invisible to agreement metrics and moves focus onto ring-symmetric scatterers. This is my reasoning, supported by the through-wall result that wrong wall parameters degrade CF (Burkholder-Browne), which implies CF is sensitive to geometry error only when a real target exists; when only the shell is coherent, the metric stays high.
- Central bias: any alignment-based aggregate (CF, semblance, SLSC at short lag) gets a high floor in the central pixels for ring-symmetric residuals (see the earlier note, section (a) inferences). The project's adipose-only result (CF about 3x higher near centre) is the empirical evidence. Hence every metric above should be z-scored against the target-free null at the same r/R before being used.
- Bright clutter: sharpness metrics (entropy, kurtosis, Gini, PSLR) reward the brightest compact object, which here is the shell residual; restrict the evaluation mask to the breast interior and mask the shell ring (r > R - delta).
- Product or ratio constructions of such indices inherit the failures (see earlier selective-classification note); prefer ranking-based, phantom-grouped OOF evaluation.

### Gaps
- No source that explicitly reports central bias of coherence indices, or tests them in a ring/near-field breast geometry.
- Evidence on SLSC/LOC failure with strong coherent clutter (e.g. specular/shell echo) not found.

## Shortlist of implementable, ground-truth-free candidates not yet in the repo (prioritized; all computed from scan, adipose reference scans and reconstruction)
1. Reference-null z-score or empirical p-value of peak amplitude / CF, conditioned on r/R (Q5). Cheap, and attacks the central/shell bias directly.
2. Sector-semblance and sector-peak spread across angular sub-apertures (Q3), with jackknife variance over position blocks. Extension of the existing half-split cue (AUROC about 0.59-0.60).
3. LOC / SLSC over neighbouring antenna positions at the peak pixel, converted to channel SNR via LOC/(1-LOC) and z-scored against the null (Q1).
4. Focus metrics computed inside the breast disc (Gini, entropy, excess half-power area A_hp/A_PSF) and, more usefully, their gain when scanning shell-centre/radius/permittivity (SAR-style autofocus objective curvature) (Q2).
5. Multi-beamformer peak disagreement (DAS, DMAS, CF-DAS) (Q4); expected weak because bias is shared.
