# Image-based tumour size estimation in microwave breast imaging (DAS / DMAS / CF) and related fields

Method note: about 22 web calls. Earlier notes (`Reliability metrics for microwave imaging\mbi_confidence_metrics.md`, `RRS MBI reliability score fixes\*`) were read and are not repeated; the f1/f focal metrics and SCR/SMR/TFRR definitions from the 2023 review are taken as known. Main finding up front: the literature on size estimation FROM A RADAR/DAS IMAGE is almost empty. The 2023 review did not identify any paper that estimates tumour size from images (visible part, first ~100k of 275k characters). Everything below that is marked UNVERIFIED comes from search snippets/abstracts or my own background knowledge. Several full texts (BRIGID TARA PDF, ACES 2016 PDF) could not be fetched (HTTP 403 / DNS error).

## 1. Which MBI papers estimate tumour size from the image, with which estimator, and how accurate is it?

### Takeaway
For radar-type DAS/DMAS/CF images, I found no paper that reports a size-error figure (mm) for an image-derived estimator (FWHM, threshold area, equivalent diameter, Otsu, edges). FWHM is used only as a descriptive "extent of response" metric, and the field's own review warns that it need not reflect the tumour. The only quantitative size-vs-truth results found are for the clinical Wavelia system (ellipsoid fit to a ROI, r = 0.70 with histology, errors of 2 to 26 mm in the 3 cases shown) and for a signal-based (not image-based) UWB empirical model (92 % "accuracy", no mm error available).

### Cited Findings
- The 2023 review (Reimer and Pistorius, Sensors 23(11):5123) says FWHM area has been used as an image-quality metric in refs 101, 114, 189, 190; the authors say it "does not clearly describe the quality of the image". Its best use is described as the "extent of the highest-intensity response". FWHM "may be expected to be approximately the same size as the tumour", but a homogeneous breast with a small fibroglandular inclusion can give a similar FWHM; healthy and tumour-containing breasts can give similar FWHM; it may not be limited to a tumour response. Footnote: FWHM may mean the area/volume above 50 % of the maximum, or a 1-D FWHM. — [PMC10255334](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10255334/)
- Same review: the visible text gives no quantitative SCR-vs-tumour-diameter relation and no LE values in mm; image resolution, noise and artifacts are listed as unaddressed. SCR and SMR depend on breast size and density and need the tumour location. The MAX/AVG (SMR-type) metric failed for a 4 cm carcinoma: "the extremely large size of such carcinoma leads to a low Max/Avg" (ref 132). Smallest detected phantom lesion 3 mm, smallest patient lesion 4 mm. — [PMC10255334](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10255334/)
- Wavelia first-in-human study (Moloney et al., Br J Radiol 2021;94:20210907, 24 women, 11 invasive cancers): size = longest axis of an ellipsoid fitted to the persistent lesion ROI, taking the larger of two density-assumption ranges (W1 10-60 %, W2 20-50 % fibroglandular). MBI size estimate more accurate than conventional imaging (mammography/US) in 6 of 9 detected cancers; radiologists judged MBI size accurate in 76.5 %; correlation with histology r = 0.704 (MBI) vs 0.65 (conventional), "too small for meaningful statistics". Examples: 62.9 mm vs 65 mm histology; 42.2 vs 35 mm; 26.6 vs 52 mm (a 2x underestimate). Two ductal cancers (10 and 9.5 mm) were missed. Lesions under 10 mm are a stated limitation; a validated detection needed at least 1 cm3. — [PMC8631021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8631021/)
- Kurnaz, Alsharif, Cheema, "Determination of the breast cancer tumor diameter using a UWB microwave antenna system", Sigma J. Eng. Nat. Sci. 41(5):999-1012, 2023: 1-12 GHz monopole ring, backscatter analysis of a breast model, tumours 1-35 mm, empirical model with "92.28 % accuracy". This is a signal-level empirical model, not an image estimator; the metric behind "accuracy" is not defined in the abstract; no mm error found. UNVERIFIED beyond the abstract. — [Ulster record](https://pure.ulster.ac.uk/en/publications/determination-of-the-breast-cancer-tumor-diameter-using-a-uwb-mic/)
- Monostatic UWB prototype (Medical Physics 2020, via catalogue snippet): 13 benign + 13 malignant tumour models, sizes 13-40 mm, classical classifiers on signals; kNN 96.2 % benign-vs-malignant in a homogeneous phantom. Classification, not size; no grouped validation reported in the snippet. UNVERIFIED. — [record](https://ersearch2.cvtisr.sk/vufind/SummonRecord/FETCH-LOGICAL-c3214-6036b51e509ef0737ef36b5978adf87fdf494412d1e4a15b4bdae4f9b0e867c13/Details)
- Cross-correlated back projection (PMC3035806): FWHM was "derived along both axes" of the imaged peak (procedure not described); SCR defined as P_peak / mean power outside the -3 dB peak area. Single 7 mm diameter tumour phantom (eps_r 10, sigma 0.3 S/m at 6 GHz) in soybean oil (eps_r 2.6), 1-12 GHz VNA sweep. DAS FWHM (x, y) = 6.3 x 2.8 mm for a point scatterer in simulation (5-element array); experimental DAS 6.9-10.8 mm (x) and 2.7-2.9 mm (y) for 4-24 antennas; TCC 3.4-4.4 and 1.3-2.4 mm. No tumour-size analysis. — [PMC3035806](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3035806/)
- Tomography side: a JoVE 2025 article on a distorted-Born-iterative approach on 24 patients reports 86 % accuracy for tumours at least 1 cm and 58 % when tumours under 1 cm are included, but these are detection figures, not size. A patent disclosure states the initial image of a tumour embedded in higher-permittivity tissue is usually larger than the actual tumour and proposes iterative permittivity estimation to shrink it (no error figures; a patent, not validation). I found no peer-reviewed Born/DBIM/CSI study quantifying tumour-size error in mm. UNVERIFIED (snippets). — [JoVE](https://www.jove.com/t/69288/clinical-imaging-of-microwave-mammography), [patent](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12569158)
- Adjacent fields (snippets only, UNVERIFIED): ultrasound sizing is threshold-dependent (lesion areas computed at iso-contour levels between -2 and -6 dB), and ultrasound beamforming overestimates kidney stone size by up to 2-3 mm ([source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3051355/table/T1) for contours; stone claim from [par.nsf.gov](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10400460)). GPR: Giannakis et al. (IEEE GRSL 2022) report that hyperbola fitting cannot reliably recover target radius together with velocity (non-uniqueness; a 2 % velocity error can give up to 500 % radius error); older work (Shihab et al.) claims within 1 % on synthetic data. Conflict is real, conditions differ (synthetic vs lab). — [Northumbria](https://researchportal.northumbria.ac.uk/en/publications/on-the-limitations-of-hyperbola-fitting-for-estimating-the-radius/)

### Inferences
- Candidate estimators implementable on a reconstructed image and reference scans (all my own formulations, none validated in MBI): (a) FWHM area A at 50 % of peak and equivalent radius r_eq = sqrt(A/pi); (b) same at -3, -6 dB or fractions 0.3-0.7 of peak to show threshold sensitivity; (c) connected-component area containing the peak after Otsu thresholding inside the breast mask; (d) 1-D FWHM along range and cross-range separately, then a quadrature "deconvolution" r = 0.5*sqrt(W_meas^2 - W_psf^2) with W_psf taken from a point-target/adipose-reference scan (a standard Gaussian-width subtraction, background knowledge). All will be dominated by the PSF for 5-15 mm radii (see section 3).
- Since no accuracy exists for these in the literature, any claim in the project that image size predicts tumour size would be novel and must be tested with grouped validation.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed mm-level size error for DAS/DMAS/CF image estimators found. Not retrieved: O'Loughlin et al. BRIGID papers (EMBC 2018 and Sensors 2021, snippet says FWHM in mm is tabulated), Kurnaz full text, ACES 2016 full text, Cambridge 2025 Table 2.
- No Born/DBIM/CSI tumour-size error study found.
- Otsu / edge-detection size estimation in MBI: nothing found.

## 2. How do peak amplitude, SCR and SMR scale with tumour diameter?

### Takeaway
Evidence is thin and not monotone-in-general: the only direct statements found are (i) tumour response grows with size but "in an oscillatory manner" at a given band (ACES 2016 abstract), (ii) SCR/SMR fall as breast heterogeneity rises, FWHM shows no clear trend (BRIGID), (iii) very large lesions can lower MAX/AVG-type contrast. I did not find a table of SCR vs 10/20/30 mm for UM-BMID Gen-1/2.

### Cited Findings
- UM-BMID structure: Gen-1 and Gen-2 tumour diameters 10, 20, 30 mm; Gen-3 has 20 phantom geometries scanned with tumours of 10, 15, 20, 25, 30 mm (200 scans, 1-9 GHz per the summary table), over 1250 scans total. Source paper: Reimer, Krenkevich, Pistorius, EuCAP 2020. (The Gen-3 band 1-9 GHz is as printed in a catalogue summary; UNVERIFIED.) — [IEEE DataPort](https://ieee-dataport.org/node/4383), [arXiv 2304.10386](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.10386)
- ACES Journal vol 31 no 2 (Feb 2016) parametric study of confocal microwave imaging (analytic + simulation): tumour response grows with tumour size at a given frequency band but in an oscillatory fashion. Abstract only; period and numbers not seen. UNVERIFIED. — [ACES PDF link](https://journals.riverpublishers.com/index.php/ACES/article/download/10219/8523/31901)
- O'Loughlin et al., "Effects of Interpatient Variance on Microwave Breast Images: Experimental Evaluation", EMBC 2018, BRIGID phantoms: table of FWHM (mm), max amplitude, SCR, SMR (dB); SCR, SMR and max amplitude generally fall as heterogeneity rises, FWHM shows no clear trend; tumour max amplitude spans -54.9 to -24.5 dB, highest at lowest glandular content and for the least spiculated tumour; authors conclude tumour and no-tumour images are hard to distinguish with existing metrics. Seen via abstract/snippet and search summaries only; whether the size dependence is tabulated is unconfirmed. UNVERIFIED. — [Galway record](https://research.universityofgalway.ie/en/publications/effects-of-interpatient-variance-on-microwave-breast-images-exper-2/), [TARA PDF (403 on fetch)](https://www.tara.tcd.ie/bitstreams/ee087853-24f0-4727-a807-fb94e27378ad/download)
- Review: MAX/AVG underestimates large lesions (4 cm carcinoma). — [PMC10255334](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10255334/)
- Bristol multistatic radar: focusing improved SCR by 2.65 dB (10 mm tumour) and 4.4 dB (7 mm); correlation methods improve SCR by at least 10 dB over DAS (snippet). UNVERIFIED. — [PMC3035806](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3035806/)

### Inferences
- Physical expectation (background knowledge, UNVERIFIED): for a target smaller than the resolution cell the coherent peak scales with target volume / cross-section (roughly r^3 in Rayleigh regime, then flattening into a resonant, oscillating regime at ka around 1 as in the oscillation note); beyond the cell the peak saturates while area grows. With attenuation, peak also falls with depth, so size and depth are confounded. In an SCR defined by tumour max over clutter max, SCR can saturate or fall for large tumours if clutter also grows; so SCR is a poor monotone size proxy.
- The project dataset (Gen-2 with 10, 20, 30 mm diameters, i.e. radii 5, 10, 15 mm) is exactly the regime where peak saturation and PSF-limited width matter; a within-dataset test of peak, SCR, FWHM vs true diameter is the only way to get this relation. That has not been published as far as I could find.

### Gaps
- No table of SCR/SMR vs diameter for UM-BMID Gen-1/2/3; Reimer 2021 numeric results are only in figures.
- ACES 2016 and BRIGID full text not read.

## 3. Resolution limits of DAS in 1-8 GHz, and blooming, speckle, deconvolution

### Takeaway
Measured experimental DAS resolution in breast-sized systems is 12 mm or worse (bed system 2-9 GHz: 12.4 +/- 0.5 mm by MTF, 29.5 +/- 0.7 mm by time-domain FWHM), larger than the 10 mm diameter tumours and comparable to the 20 mm ones, so image width for 5 mm and 10 mm radius tumours is expected to be PSF-limited. Deconvolution (CLEAN, Gabor) exists for breast radar but no size-accuracy results were found.

### Cited Findings
- Reimer et al. 2025, IJMWT, "Image quality analysis and comparison of three radar-based breast microwave sensing systems" (doi 10.1017/S1759078725000376): three resolution methods: (1) time-domain FWHM of a rod via ICZT, d = FWHM_t x c/2 (c in air); (2) PSF of DAS images of single rods converted to an MTF, described as best-case without scattering; (3) two-target separation where the intensity between targets is below 90 % of the smaller peak. Bed-based system 2.0-9.0 GHz: MTF 12.4 +/- 0.5 mm, time-domain FWHM 29.5 +/- 0.7 mm; bench-top 0.7-8.0 GHz worse than bed despite larger bandwidth; portable 0.7-4.4 GHz worst. Bench-top data SNR 22 +/- 2 dB vs image SNR 14 +/- 4 dB (clutter/reconstruction artifacts). Intensity of bed and bench systems dropped by about 2x from 0 to 7 cm target position (affects resolution measurement). The text's conclusion writes 12.4 +/- 5 mm, inconsistent with 0.5 in Results. Table 2 values for the other systems not readable. — [Cambridge core reader](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/CD816E28EAFDCD815FC1108BAA40AC62/core-reader)
- Tumour-phantom FWHM, DAS, 1-12 GHz sweep, in a lossy low-permittivity oil: about 7-11 mm (x) by about 3 mm (y) for a 7 mm tumour/point target; correlation methods cut FWHM by 40-50 %. FWHM depends on antenna count in the 4-24 range (10.8 mm at 4, about 7 mm at 8-24). — [PMC3035806](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3035806/)
- Wider bandwidth gives shorter pulse and better resolution (3-11 GHz best among bands tested in a 2020 study); microwave methods "typically resolve only a few millimetres" limited by bandwidth and loss (snippet). UNVERIFIED. — [dergipark](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/1023588)
- Deconvolution: a patent describes the subtractive CLEAN approach (remove point responses in descending amplitude order) as applicable to coherent radar imaging; nonstationary (Gabor) deconvolution on UWB breast data improved image sensitivity in a preliminary test (University of Calgary thesis / 2014 paper); McMaster lecture notes describe measuring the system PSF from a calibration object. No Richardson-Lucy MBI paper found. No size-error results for any of these. UNVERIFIED (snippets). — [Calgary](https://prism.ucalgary.ca/handle/11023/4163), [McMaster notes](https://www.ece.mcmaster.ca/faculty/nikolova/IntroMicrowaveImaging/IntroMicrowImagingDay2_LinearInversionMethods.pdf)
- Ultrasound analogy: sized area depends on the iso-contour level (-2 to -6 dB). — [PMC3051355](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3051355/table/T1) (UNVERIFIED)

### Inferences (my calculations, background knowledge, not cited)
- Range resolution of the pulse envelope: dR = c / (2 B sqrt(eps_r)). For B = 7 GHz (1-8 GHz): vacuum 21.4 mm; eps_r = 4: 10.7 mm; eps_r = 9: 7.1 mm; eps_r about 15-20 (fibroglandular/skin-adjacent): about 5-5.5 mm. The envelope FWHM is of that order, but the matched-filter image of a real tumour also contains the dispersion of lossy tissue, which widens it.
- Cross-range for a nearly full circular aperture at centre frequency f0 about 4.5 GHz in eps_r 9: lambda about 22 mm, cross-range FWHM about lambda/2 about 11 mm near the centre, worsening with offset from the centre and limited by antenna count and a single-port-pair bistatic geometry. So a 5 mm radius (10 mm diameter) tumour is at or below the resolution cell, 10 mm radius (20 mm) is about 2 cells, 15 mm radius (30 mm) is the only one clearly above. Expect FWHM roughly sqrt(W_psf^2 + d^2) in the simple Gaussian model: widths of 10-15 mm for 10 mm tumours and 20-30 mm for 30 mm tumours; the sensitivity d(FWHM)/d(diameter) is therefore small for small tumours, which would compress the size signal; the Reimer 12.4 mm MTF resolution supports this order of magnitude.
- A localization error or delay-model error blurs the coherent sum, so wrong-delay beamforming widens and lowers the blob; this couples size and localization quality (and is a possible source of size bias in the project's broken pipeline). Not found in the literature as a quantified result.
- The CF-weighted image is narrower and non-linear (peak region pushed to a smaller area at high coherence), so threshold area after CF understates true extent; DMAS similarly narrower and with artifacts. Not quantified in the sources found.

### Gaps
- No primary source found for blooming/size bias of DAS vs DMAS vs CF in MBI, or for depth/density dependence of blob width. No Richardson-Lucy MBI paper. Cross-range formula for a circular aperture not confirmed against a source.
- The key 2025 paper's Table 2 (all three systems) not readable.

## 4. Dependence on depth, density, shell/skin clutter, beamformer and threshold

### Takeaway
Only fragments: FWHM no clear trend with heterogeneity (BRIGID), SCR/SMR drop with density and breast size, signal intensity falls with position (2x over 7 cm), the review says the FWHM response is not tumour-specific, and the DAS hot-spot artifact (earlier note) is a clutter blob that has FWHM too.

### Cited Findings
- Density and heterogeneity: SCR, SMR, max amplitude generally fall as heterogeneity rises; FWHM no clear trend (O'Loughlin 2018, UNVERIFIED snippet). — [Galway record](https://research.universityofgalway.ie/en/publications/effects-of-interpatient-variance-on-microwave-breast-images-exper-2/)
- Depth/position: intensity of the bed and bench systems fell about 2x from 0 to 7 cm. — [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/CD816E28EAFDCD815FC1108BAA40AC62/core-reader)
- Fibroglandular contrast can be as small as 1.1:1 and fibroglandular responses can fall inside the FWHM. — [PMC10255334](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10255334/)
- Wavelia handles tissue heterogeneity by repeating reconstruction over several fibroglandular-fraction assumptions and fitting an ellipsoid to the persistent region (size estimate depends on that range choice, larger of W1/W2 used). — [PMC8631021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8631021/)
- Threshold: size depends on the iso-contour level in ultrasound (-2 to -6 dB). The review states that diagnostic estimates depend strongly on the SCR threshold (earlier note).

### Inferences
- A threshold-sensitivity curve (area vs fraction of peak) can be computed per scan; a flat curve (area insensitive to threshold) indicates a compact, well-defined peak, a steep one indicates a broad clutter-dominated peak. This is my own suggestion, no source.
- Because shell/skin clutter sits within the same image as the tumour, any area-based estimator must mask the shell ring; the project's own finding (blob extraction picked up the focused shell ring) shows how a peak finder can pick the wrong object.

### Gaps
- No quantitative depth, density or beamformer dependence of blob width found. No paper on delay-model-error inflation of size.

## 5. Tumour size classification (small vs large) and trivial baselines

### Takeaway
I found no MBI paper that classifies tumour size from reconstructed images with grouped validation and reports the majority-class baseline. The few classification results found are for signals or for benign vs malignant, and none reports a trivial baseline in the snippets.

### Cited Findings
- Medical Physics 2020 monostatic prototype: benign vs malignant kNN 96.2 % (homogeneous phantom), tumour models 13-40 mm; split strategy and baseline not visible. UNVERIFIED. — [record](https://ersearch2.cvtisr.sk/vufind/SummonRecord/FETCH-LOGICAL-c3214-6036b51e509ef0737ef36b5978adf87fdf494412d1e4a15b4bdae4f9b0e867c13/Details)
- Review: ML studies frequently used multiple measurements of the same phantom and data leakage occurred in several; only ref 120 met basic ML standards (earlier note). — [PMC10255334](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10255334/)
- Sogeti Labs overview mentions size regression as a task and flags that tumour size and contrast effects are confounded (blog; low evidence). — [Sogeti](https://labs.sogeti.com/three-tasks-three-learning-strategies-in-microwave-breast-imaging/)
- A 2025 arXiv paper (PMC12349357, attention-based sub-band selection for localization and size estimation) exists, simulation only; not read in detail (earlier note). — [PMC12349357](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12349357)

### Inferences
- With Gen-2 having three diameters (10/20/30 mm), the trivial 3-class baseline is the class frequency (about 33 % if balanced; must be computed from the data), and grouped splits must be by phantom (the same phantom is scanned with several tumour sizes/locations). Any size classifier should be reported against that, plus a "predict from tumour position/depth only" control, since depth confounds amplitude.

### Gaps
- No grouped-validation size-classification result with trivial baseline found; the Medical Physics and arXiv papers should be read in full before relying on them.
