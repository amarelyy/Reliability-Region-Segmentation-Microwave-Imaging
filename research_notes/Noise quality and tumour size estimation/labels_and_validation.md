# Labels and validation for a three-notion "reliable scan" detector (R1 clean, R2 localization, R3 size)

Method note: about 17 tool calls. Earlier notes `Reliability metrics for microwave imaging/pitfalls_confounding_power.md` and `selective_prediction_uq.md` were read first and are NOT repeated (clustered AUROC CIs, Holm/BH, forking paths, grouped CV, Janes-Pepe AROC, conformal risk control, ridge vs rank-mean, risk-coverage and AURC are already there; I refer to them as "earlier note"). Almost every source here was seen only as a search summary or a fetched abstract; full texts of Baccianella 2009 (HTTP 403), Cardoso & Sousa 2011 (HTTP 403) and Amigo et al. (PDF bytes unparsed) were NOT readable. Anything seen only at that level is marked UNVERIFIED. Items marked "Inference" are my own reasoning or arithmetic, not read in a source. Dataset facts used (from the assignment): about 504 scans, about 66 phantoms, R2 positives 84, radii 5/10/15 mm at about 32/40/29 %.

Headline for the writer: (1) the literature gives good tools for agreement and ordinal metrics but almost no tumour-size tolerance for microwave imaging; (2) with only three radii, "within one class" or "within 5 mm" is vacuous for a constant "always 10 mm" predictor, so the R3 label must be "exact class" (or a continuous error under 2.5 mm) and must be compared with a constant predictor that already gets about 40 % accuracy; (3) I found NO published framework for joint selective prediction across several reliability notions, so the multi-notion design below is my synthesis; (4) the noise estimator should be validated by injection and recovery, with the label produced independently of the predictor's features.

## Q1. How do imaging papers define "correct size" / "size agreement", what tolerances are typical, and how should R1, R2, R3 labels be defined?

### Takeaway
Clinical size-agreement work uses (i) Bland-Altman bias plus 95 % limits of agreement judged against a pre-set clinically acceptable difference, (ii) ICC for reproducibility, (iii) within-X mm or X % tolerance (RECIST: 20 % and at least 5 mm absolute), and (iv) weighted kappa for categorised sizes. Typical limits of agreement between imaging and pathology size are much wider than 5 mm. For a three-level radius, only exact-class agreement is non-trivial; "within one class" is always true for a middle-class prediction.

### Cited Findings
- Bland-Altman (Lancet 1986, i:307-310) is the standard method-agreement analysis; the paper gives a formula for the CI of the limits of agreement and says sample size should be settled in advance — [first page / search summary, Hanley's copy](https://jhanley.biostat.mcgill.ca/bios601/Surveys/BlandAltman1986.pdf), [Bland FAQ on sample size](https://www-users.york.ac.uk/~mb55/meas/sizemeth). Full 1986 text not read: UNVERIFIED in detail.
- Bland's note: the standard error of a limit of agreement is roughly sqrt(3 s^2 / n) (s = SD of the differences); n = 100 gives a 95 % CI of about plus or minus 0.34 s and n = 200 about plus or minus 0.24 s — [Bland FAQ](https://www-users.york.ac.uk/~mb55/meas/sizemeth).
- A power-based alternative to precision-based sizing exists (Lu et al. 2016; MedCalc example minimum n = 83) — [MedCalc manual](https://www.medcalc.org/manual/sample-size-bland-altman.php). Seen as manual text only.
- Tumour-size agreement examples show wide limits: MRI vs pathology after neoadjuvant therapy pooled mean difference 0.1 cm with 95 % LoA about -4.2 to 4.4 (the unit was reported as cm in the search summary; this looks implausible and may be mm; UNVERIFIED); mammography vs histology mean difference 1.26 mm with LoA -22.29 to 24.73 mm; one ultrasound study pre-set a maximum allowed difference and judged agreement by whether the LoA fell inside it — search summary of [PMC3776985](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3776985), [BMC Cancer 13:328](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1186/1471-2407-13-328) and neighbours. Individual papers not opened: UNVERIFIED.
- RECIST 1.1 progression: a 20 % increase in the sum of target diameters plus an absolute increase of at least 5 mm, to avoid overcalling in small lesions — search summary of [PMC5737828](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/5737828) and neighbours (UNVERIFIED, not opened).
- Reproducibility: in a CT test-retest study of lung tumours (scans within 15 minutes), 84 % of measurements changed by within plus or minus 10 %, and apparent diameter changes of 1 to 2 mm were common; smaller lesions had greater percent variability — search summary of results at [PMC4429002](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4429002) / [PMC3157977](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3157977) (which one carries the figure was not confirmed; UNVERIFIED).
- ICC: choice of form (model, type, definition) follows from the study design and should be reported with software; common benchmarks are below 0.50 poor, 0.50-0.75 moderate, 0.75-0.90 good, above 0.90 excellent; judge by the 95 % CI — Koo & Li 2016, J Chiropr Med 15(2):155-163, [PMC4913118](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/4913118) (search summary; the CI advice not confirmed in the text). Updated guidance: ten Hove, Jorgensen, van der Ark 2022, Psychological Methods ([VU record](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/updated-guidelines-on-selecting-an-intraclass-correlation-coeffic/)); abstract level.
- Weighted kappa: quadratically weighted kappa is the most common weighted kappa for ordinal scales, but for tables with an odd number of categories it can be independent of the centre cell under some base-rate conditions, so Warrens (Psychometrika 2012) recommends linear weights for a single index — [Cambridge abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/psychometrika/article/some-paradoxical-results-for-the-quadratically-weighted-kappa/BFBA88B72EF53081B4F11C26C2A4A50A), search summary.
- Kappa depends on prevalence (Byrt, Bishop, Carlin 1993; Feinstein & Cicchetti 1990 "high agreement but low kappa"), with a counter-view that this dependence is not a problem (Vach 2005, J Clin Epidemiol 58:655-661) — references seen only inside [Doewes et al., EDM 2023](https://educationaldatamining.org/EDM2023/proceedings/2023.EDM-long-papers.9/index.html) summary; the originals were not opened: UNVERIFIED.

### Inferences (labels; all mine unless stated)
- R1 (clean): there is no direct label. Define it by a measurable surrogate that is independent of the predictor features (see Q4): (a) residual energy of the scan after subtraction of its own adipose-only reference, versus the distribution of the same residual for pairs of adipose-only scans; (b) for injection experiments, the known injected sigma. A binary "clean" label should be a threshold on that continuous quantity fixed in advance (for example the 75th percentile of the reference-pair distribution), and the continuous version should also be analysed (Spearman, not only AUROC), since a binary cut removes information.
- R2 (localization): binary "LE <= tol" (tol = 15 mm already used; 84 positives). Add a sensitivity analysis at tol = r + 5 mm (radius-aware tolerance, as the Gen-3 paper in the earlier note uses r + 0.5 cm) and the continuous LE as a secondary target (Spearman), because a hard cut near a mass of LE values near 15 mm is fragile.
- R3 (size): with radii 5, 10, 15 mm the class spacing is 5 mm, so a "tolerance of 5 mm" equals "within one class". A constant predictor "10 mm" is within 5 mm for 100 % of scans, so that label is useless. Defensible labels, in order: (i) exact class match (equivalently, for a continuous estimate, absolute error under 2.5 mm, half the class spacing); (ii) absolute error under 5 mm only if the estimate is continuous and the comparison constant is applied to the same rule; (iii) ordinal error |class difference| in {0, 1, 2} as a graded label. Never use "within one class" as a success definition.
- Bland-Altman is an appropriate secondary report for R3 if the size estimate is continuous (mean bias and 95 % LoA versus the true radius); with only three truth levels, plot the estimate per true level (a strip plot) rather than a classical difference-against-mean plot, because the truth is discrete and the plot will show three vertical bands. State the LoA beside the class spacing (5 mm): LoA wider than plus or minus 5 mm means classes cannot be told apart.
- The sample-size arithmetic for LoA (Bland): n = 504 gives a CI of about plus or minus 1.96 * sqrt(3/504) = plus or minus 0.15 s on each limit (my arithmetic from the cited formula); phantom clustering (about 7.6 scans each) makes the effective n smaller, so quote about plus or minus 0.2 s or use a phantom-cluster bootstrap.

### Gaps
- No source found giving a tolerance for tumour SIZE estimates in microwave breast imaging; the clinical figures above are for CT/MRI/US/mammography versus pathology and are not transferable.
- ICC for a discrete three-level truth versus a continuous estimate is not a standard application; no source found.
- Original Bland-Altman 1986, Byrt 1993, Feinstein & Cicchetti 1990, Vach 2005 and Cohen 1968 were not opened.

## Q2. Evaluating a "size correct" classifier against majority and median baselines; pitfalls of three balanced classes

### Takeaway
For ordinal targets use ordinal-aware errors (MAE, macro-averaged MAE) and chance-corrected agreement (linear weighted kappa), always next to the constant-predictor values. With sizes at about 32/40/29 %, a constant "10 mm" predictor has accuracy about 40 %, balanced accuracy 1/3, kappa 0, and within-one-class accuracy 100 %.

### Cited Findings
- Ordinal classification has ordered classes without meaningful numeric distances; measures designed for nominal classes or for regression do not capture the true dimension of the error; Cardoso & Sousa (Int. J. Pattern Recognit. Artif. Intell. 25(8):1173-1195, 2011, doi 10.1142/S0218001411009093) propose a confusion-matrix-based measure (OCI) and criticise rank-correlation approaches because they lose information about absolute class values — [INESC TEC record](https://repositorio.inesctec.pt/items/7df8325e-7f56-4946-9ff6-ef6a6e91e17f/full), [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1142/s0218001411009093). Abstract level; the search tool itself said the journal name was not verified.
- Baccianella, Esuli, Sebastiani (2009) propose macro-averaged versions of MAE and RMSE for ordinal classification under class imbalance: compute MAE per true class and average over classes, so every class has equal weight and a trivial majority-class classifier is not rewarded; lower is better; used as the main ordinal measure in SemEval-2016 and 2017 Task 4 — [search summaries, SemEval 2016 task paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.01973), [CNR record](https://iris.cnr.it/retrieve/5bcf86c7-cd68-4884-93b5-ff86095082ec/prod_91979-doc_199135.pdf) (PDF fetch returned 403). UNVERIFIED: formulas not read.
- Gaudette & Japkowicz (Canadian AI 2009, LNCS 5549) evaluate measures for ordinal classification, including accuracy, MAE, MSE and AUC-style measures — [Springer record](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/978-3-642-01818-3_25); abstract only: UNVERIFIED for their recommendations.
- Amigo, Gonzalo, Mizzaro, Carrillo-de-Albornoz (ACL 2020): common metrics either disregard order (per-class precision/recall) or assume known distances (MAE); they propose the Closeness Evaluation Measure from measurement theory — [arXiv abstract 2006.01245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.01245). A follow-up meta-evaluation by Sakai (ACL 2021) exists — [ACL 2021 long.214](https://preview.aclanthology.org/fix_video/2021.acl-long.214.pdf), snippet only.
- Quadratic weights have the odd-category anomaly above; linear weights are preferred for a single agreement index (Warrens 2012; see Q1).
- Kappa is affected by class prevalence; with many classes of unequal size the prevalence index has no comprehensive formula for 3x3 or larger tables — [EDM 2023](https://educationaldatamining.org/EDM2023/proceedings/2023.EDM-long-papers.9/index.html) summary.

### Inferences (arithmetic is mine, from the 32/40/29 split; the split sums to 101 %, treat as rounded)
- Constant predictor "10 mm" (it is both the majority class and the median): accuracy about 0.40; balanced accuracy 1/3; Cohen kappa 0 and linear weighted kappa 0 (any constant prediction has zero agreement beyond chance, because the predicted marginal is degenerate); within-one-class accuracy 1.0; MAE about 0.61 * 5 mm = 3.05 mm; macro-MAE = (5 + 0 + 5)/3 = 3.3 mm. Constant "5 mm": accuracy 0.32, MAE about 0.40 * 5 + 0.29 * 10 = 4.9 mm. So the bar to beat is MAE 3.05 mm (micro) and macro-MAE 3.3 mm, not zero.
- Report for R3: exact accuracy minus 0.40 (accuracy gain over majority) with a phantom-cluster bootstrap CI; macro-MAE and micro-MAE with their constant-predictor values; linear weighted kappa; balanced accuracy; and the 3x3 confusion matrix. Skill score form: 1 - MAE_model / MAE_constant (my choice, analogous to the earlier note's skill score for LE).
- Pitfalls of three roughly balanced classes: (a) accuracy is low-baseline-friendly: 40 % is the floor, so a reported "55 % accuracy" is a 15-point gain, not majority-beating by "more than half"; (b) the middle class is hardest for a model whose error is symmetric, and the extreme classes benefit from range compression: a regressor that shrinks toward the mean gets the middle class right and the extremes wrong, producing a decent micro-MAE with near-zero macro-MAE improvement; use macro measures to expose this; (c) with only three levels, ordinal metrics barely differ from nominal ones; weighted kappa then reduces to a combination of exact and adjacent agreement and offers little beyond MAE; (d) confusion between 5 and 15 mm (two-class error) is the only error with a real consequence, so report the count of two-class errors separately; (e) sizes may be confounded with depth/position and with phantom (repeat scans share a radius), so evaluate within phantom-grouped folds only.
- Power for R3 (my arithmetic, binomial, design effect about 2 from clustering assumed): SE of accuracy at n = 504 about sqrt(0.4 * 0.6 / 504) = 0.022, times sqrt(2) about 0.031; so to separate from 0.40 with 80 % power and alpha 0.05 the true accuracy needs to be about 0.40 + 2.8 * 0.031 = 0.49 or higher. A model with accuracy 0.45 cannot be validated at this n.

### Gaps
- No source states a decision rule "model must beat the median predictor by X" for ordinal classifiers; the baseline-gap approach is standard practice, not a cited rule.
- Formulas of macro-MAE, OCI and Closeness measure not read from full text (fetches failed); verify before citing a formula.
- Behaviour of weighted kappa with three levels and balanced classes was not found in any source.

## Q3. Combining or comparing several reliability notions; avoiding circularity

### Takeaway
Treat the three notions as separate endpoints first (one AUROC per notion, each with its own constant/metadata baseline), then measure agreement among the labels (phi/tetrachoric or Spearman), and only then define joint labels. I found no published framework for joint selective prediction with multiple abstention criteria; the single-criterion tools in the earlier note (risk-coverage, Learn-then-Test) can be extended, but that extension is mine.

### Cited Findings
- Selective prediction literature found is single-output: SelectiveNet's risk-coverage curve and the two problem variants (fix risk and maximise coverage, or fix coverage and minimise risk) — [SelectiveNet](https://www.arxiv-vanity.com/papers/1901.09192/), AAAI 2023 paper at [ojs.aaai.org](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/35094/37249); selective prediction-set models with coverage guarantees — Feng et al. 2023, Biometrics, [arXiv 1906.05473](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.05473v1); all search-summary level. A search for multi-output / joint-abstention work returned none: UNVERIFIED absence.
- Learn-then-Test casts risk control as multiple-hypothesis testing with finite-sample guarantees — [arXiv 2110.01052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01052) (from the earlier note; abstract level). This is the natural carrier for several simultaneous risk constraints (one hypothesis family over a threshold grid and over notions).
- Rank-correlation agreement loses information about absolute class values for ordinal data — Cardoso & Sousa (see Q2), so label agreement for R3 should use weighted kappa or the ordinal error, not Spearman alone.

### Inferences (design; mine)
1. Primary: three separate endpoints, each with its own pre-specified AUROC and constant baseline (R1: injection recovery plus real-scan label; R2: LE <= 15 mm vs trivial centre; R3: exact class vs "always 10 mm"). Adjust across the three with Holm (family size 3, not larger).
2. Agreement between notions: report the 3x3 matrix of phi coefficients (binary labels) and Spearman (continuous versions: noise residual, LE, absolute size error), with phantom-cluster bootstrap CIs. Low correlation means one detector cannot serve all three; a single "reliability" score is then a bad construct. High correlation partly reflects shared causes (for example tumour near centre makes LE small and size error large simultaneously), so also report partial correlations controlling for true tumour radius, depth and shell radius.
3. Joint definitions: conjunctive R_all = R1 and R2 and R3 (strict; prevalence about product only if independent; with R2 prevalence 17 % and R3 exact about 40 to 50 %, prevalence of R_all will be about 5 % or less, about 25 scans, too few to validate); disjunctive or count-based (number of notions satisfied, 0 to 3) as an ordinal label; consensus (at least 2 of 3). Pre-specify one as primary-joint at most; state expected positives before looking.
4. Multi-criteria abstention: accept a scan only if score_k >= t_k for all k; to avoid a 3-dimensional threshold search with 84 positives, use a single index (the minimum of the rank-transformed scores) or require the same coverage c on each notion; report coverage versus conjunctive-risk curve along the one-parameter family (c varies). Calibrate with Learn-then-Test on phantom-grouped folds (see earlier note).
5. Circularity: if a feature is both a predictor and part of the label definition, the AUROC measures a tautology (for example R1 defined by residual energy and the detector's score using residual energy). Safeguards: (a) split-sample: compute the label on a disjoint subset of channels/frequencies/antennas from the one used by the detector; (b) use a label from independent information (adipose-only references, injected sigma, repeat scans); (c) when the detector includes the label's variable, report the "oracle" AUROC (the detector on the label's own variable) as an upper bound and discuss; (d) leave the label variable out of the composite and test whether the composite still predicts it (a direct test of whether the other features carry noise information).

### Gaps
- No published joint multi-criterion selective prediction framework located; rely on the single-criterion literature.
- No source comparing "conjunctive vs consensus" reliability definitions in imaging was found.
- Tetrachoric correlation and partial-AUROC for multiple endpoints were not searched.

## Q4. Validating the "clean of noise" notion: injection and recovery protocol

### Takeaway
Noise estimators are commonly validated by adding noise of known variance to clean data and checking bias and variance of the estimate across levels; known artefacts are overestimation at very low noise and on textured/clipped content. For scans with no clean ground truth, use (i) injected noise at known SNR into real scans as the primary test, (ii) a reference-pair residual as the natural-noise label, (iii) negative controls.

### Cited Findings
- Standard protocol: add zero-mean white Gaussian noise of several known variances to clean images and compare each estimator's output with the truth in terms of bias and variance — summary of [Noise-level estimation from single colour image, arXiv 1904.02566](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.02566).
- In that paper the ground-truth level is taken from ten noisy copies (root of the mean per-pixel variance) and is slightly smaller than the injected sigma because of clipping to [0,1]; the method slightly overestimated noise when the true level was very small — [arXiv 1904.02566](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.02566), search summary.
- Liu et al. (CVPR 2006) estimates are over-estimates of the true noise variance by construction, because the segments can contain image variations — [Liu CVPR06 PDF](https://people.csail.mit.edu/celiu/denoise/estnoise/noise.pdf), search summary.
- Pyatykh et al. PCA-based estimator discards saturated pixels and adapts to signal-dependent noise — [arXiv 1409.8230](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.8230v9.pdf), summary.
- A Poisson-Gaussian noise-fitting paper validates first on synthetic images with exactly known parameters, then on real raw data — [TIP paper (typeset)](https://typeset.io/papers/practical-poissonian-gaussian-noise-modeling-and-fitting-for-4cn04dppj4), summary.
- The searching tool found no canonical "known-variance recovery benchmark" for noise estimators: UNVERIFIED absence.

### Inferences: a protocol (mine; component ideas from the cited protocol)
1. Unit: a real scan S (complex S21, f by antenna position). Estimate its natural noise floor sigma0 from reference pairs (two adipose-only scans of the same shell design, difference / sqrt(2)); this is an independent natural-noise sample set (assumes repeated references per shell design exist; 141 references per the project notes, but pairing availability not checked).
2. Injection: add complex circular Gaussian noise with sigma_inj on a grid (for example sigma_inj / sigma0 = 0, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4), independent per frequency and antenna, at least 20 random draws per scan and level; also three non-Gaussian degradations: (a) slow drift (a low-order polynomial in position index added to phase or amplitude), (b) a clutter term (a scaled copy of another phantom's echo), (c) timing jitter (a random delay per antenna). Keep these separate: the detector may respond to one and not the others, and a single "noise" scalar is not expected to cover all.
3. Recovery metrics (pre-specify): (i) slope and intercept of estimated noise against injected total variance sigma0^2 + sigma_inj^2 (expected slope 1 in quadrature if the estimator is variance-calibrated; a non-linear but monotone relationship is acceptable for a ranking score); (ii) Spearman between estimate and injected level; (iii) the minimum detectable injected level (the level where AUROC for injected-vs-uninjected exceeds 0.8); (iv) bias at sigma_inj = 0, which exposes overestimation like that reported for textured images; (v) invariance to nuisance transforms that do not add noise (global gain, global phase rotation, small mirror or rotation of position index): the estimate must not change.
4. Negative controls: shuffled references (pair a scan with a reference from a different shell design or a different phantom): the residual should rise but the detector should not behave as noise-calibrated; shuffle antenna index within a scan; label permutation at the phantom level for any AUROC; constant-feature detector (centre-distance, antenna radius, session) as a confound test: if it recovers the injected level, the pipeline is leaking.
5. Real-data check: correlate the detector with natural noise in the reference-pair residual (second validation, weaker than injection because natural noise is mixed with drift); do not mix it with the injection result in one number.
6. Pass criteria written before running: for example, Spearman at least 0.9 on injected levels, bias at zero injection under 20 % of sigma0, nuisance transforms change the estimate by under 5 %, shuffled-reference residual separates from true-reference residual with AUROC at least 0.9.
7. Clipping/saturation: S21 is not clipped but derived images and normalised magnitudes may be; if the detector works on clipped data, define ground truth by Monte-Carlo variance (cited paper's approach), not the nominal sigma.
8. Relationship to R2/R3: injection tells you the detector measures noise; it does not tell you noise matters for localization. Test that separately: LE versus injected sigma_inj on the same scans (dose-response); if LE does not degrade, noise is not the failure mode and R1 is a poor proxy for R2.

### Gaps
- No measurement-science paper directly on noise or clutter estimation for S21 radar data was searched; the protocol is adapted from image-noise estimation work.
- Whether paired adipose-only references exist per shell design for all scans is a data question not resolved here.
- Validation by comparison with analytic noise models (e.g., VNA noise floor from instrument specs, IF bandwidth) was not searched; the available analytic noise value, if documented, should be added as a third anchor.

## Q5. Sandbox testing practice, leakage, baselines, multiplicity and pre-registration

### Takeaway
Use a pyramid: unit tests with synthetic signals, property-based and metamorphic tests for the metric code, regression tests on saved outputs, and permutation null tests as the top-level checks of the evaluation; keep metadata-only and constant baselines in every table; pre-register primary endpoints per notion.

### Cited Findings
- Scientific code often has no test oracle; metamorphic testing checks that outputs obey relations between transformed inputs, and identifying relations is the hard part — Kanewala & Bieman, Inf. Softw. Technol. 2014 (62 studies), [arXiv 1804.01954](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.01954.pdf), search summary.
- Property-based testing (Hypothesis) generates many random inputs to check a property, for example that a normalisation gives near-zero mean and unit variance; useful where expected outputs are hard to write — search summary of [engineered.at](https://engineered.at/articles/writing-robust-tests-for-data-machine-learning-pipelines), [pyvideo Hypothesis talk](https://pyvideo.org/euroscipy-2023/generating-data-frames-for-your-test-using-pandas-strategies-in-hypothesis.html); low-authority blog-level sources.
- Data-pipeline test pyramids: schema/contract checks at the base, value-level checks in the middle, regression comparisons of aggregates and distributions on top — [DEV Community post](https://dev.to/alexmercedcoder/testing-data-pipelines-what-to-validate-and-when-2hei), blog-level. The search found no source that combines these with permutation null tests in one strategy.
- Permutation tests: compute the statistic, permute, recompute; permuting the response severs its relation to all predictors — [SciPy permutation_test docs](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.permutation_test.html), [course notes](https://db7-coursenotes.github.io/ST362-STAR/Lb19-Permutation.html).
- TRIPOD+AI (BMJ 2024) updates TRIPOD for machine-learning prediction models: 27-item checklist; a draft expanded checklist includes an item asking for registration information or a statement that the study was not registered (draft on Research Square; final BMJ wording not checked, UNVERIFIED) — [EQUATOR TRIPOD page](https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/tripod/), [draft PDF](https://assets-eu.researchsquare.com/files/rs-9474749/v1/e30b93c1f5ac77214c7d0a40.pdf). TRIPOD-Cluster (BMJ 2023) adds ten items and updates 19 for clustered data — [Debray et al. 2023](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/files/187291565/DebrayT2023Transparent.pdf), summary.
- Grouped CV, nested CV, clustered AUROC, covariate-adjusted ROC, Holm/BH, minimum detectable AUROC about 0.60-0.62 at n = 504: see earlier note `pitfalls_confounding_power.md` and `selective_prediction_uq.md`.

### Inferences (mine)
- Test pyramid for metric development, with the specific tests:
  1. Unit tests on synthetic signals with known answers: a point scatterer at a known position gives LE 0; scaling all amplitudes does not change a scale-free score; adding noise of known sigma changes the noise estimate monotonically (the Q4 injection as an automated test).
  2. Property-based tests: score invariant to global phase rotation and to a global gain; score invariant to antenna order reversal combined with the matching geometry flip (a metamorphic relation); AUROC computation invariant to monotone transforms of the score; tie handling gives AUROC 0.5 for a constant score; phantom-grouped splits never put a phantom in both train and test (assert on group sets).
  3. Regression tests: store AUROCs, class counts and constant-baseline values (centre LE, majority accuracy 0.40, MAE 3.05 mm) from a frozen run in a CSV and fail if they change beyond a tolerance; also a golden test that the leakage-free split yields identical group assignments for a seed.
  4. Null tests: (a) permute success labels at the phantom level (1000 times) and check that the pipeline's AUROC distribution is centred at 0.5 with the expected SD (about 0.034 to 0.044 per the earlier simulation); (b) feed a random noise score through the full evaluation, should give 0.5; (c) "planted signal" test: a synthetic score equal to the label plus noise at a known AUROC must be recovered within its CI, which tests the evaluation code and the CI coverage together; (d) metadata-only detector (antenna radius, session, shell radius, breast size, true tumour radius or depth) trained under the same grouped CV as the actual detector.
- Baselines table (every notion): R2: predict-centre; R3: always 10 mm (majority and median), plus shuffled predictions; R1: metadata-only and constant. Add scan-quality-only predictors (for example mean amplitude, SNR of the raw scan) as the baseline for R2 and R3, to show whether the "reliability" score is more than raw scan quality. For each, report AUROC with CI and the paired phantom-cluster-bootstrap difference between the detector and the best baseline; a detector that does not beat the best metadata baseline carries no usable information beyond metadata. Metadata AUROC should be computed using the same nested grouped CV (a high-capacity learner on metadata is the stronger baseline).
- Size-stratified AUROC: stratify by true tumour radius (three levels) for R2 and R1; for R3, stratify by shell radius or depth. Report the within-stratum AUROCs and the pooled; if the pooled exceeds all strata, the pooled value is confounded (Simpson-type); use AROC from the earlier note.
- Multiplicity and power (arithmetic mine, using the assignment's counts): R2 has 84 positives of 504 (prevalence 0.167), earlier simulation gives a minimum detectable AUROC about 0.60-0.62. R3 positives equal the classifier's own accuracy times n; if the candidate size estimator reaches 0.45 to 0.50 accuracy, positives are about 230 to 250 and negatives similar, the SD of the AUROC falls (about 0.025 to 0.03 without clustering by my rough scaling, UNVERIFIED), so R3 and R1 endpoints are better powered than R2 even though the R3 improvement over the majority baseline is the hard part (Q2). The conjunctive label (about 25 positives) is not testable.
