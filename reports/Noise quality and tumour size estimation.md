# Defining and testing three kinds of scan reliability

Three different questions hide behind the phrase "reliable scan": is the scan clean (R1), is the localization error low (R2), and is the tumour size estimated correctly (R3). The literature answers none of them for UM-BMID-style data. No paper found validates a ground-truth-free scan-quality score against imaging success, and none reports an image-based tumour-size error in millimetres for DAS, DMAS or CF images, so all three notions are partly new to this project. R1 has the best-founded detectors, because noise floor, drift and clutter-rank diagnostics are standard signal processing, but the recipes are the researchers' own and have never been validated on this dataset. R3 is physically the hardest. With radii of 5, 10 and 15 mm and resolution cells of about 7 to 11 mm, a 5 mm radius tumour is at or below one cell and a 10 mm radius tumour is about two, so image width carries little size information. A rough error budget (inference) gives a per-scan separability d' of about 0.5 to 1 for 10 versus 15 mm and 1 to 3 for 5 versus 10 mm, so a coarse small-versus-large call is the most one can hope for. The R3 label must be "exact class", because a "within one class" tolerance is always true for a constant 10 mm guess, and that guess already scores about 0.40 accuracy. With 504 scans from 66 phantoms, a model needs roughly 0.49 accuracy to be separated from that baseline. The defensible design is three separately tested endpoints, each against a trivial baseline, with Holm correction over three and the ACCEPT, REJECT or INCONCLUSIVE rule of section 5. Given the project's power ceiling (84 R2 successes, minimum detectable AUROC about 0.60 to 0.62), INCONCLUSIVE is the most likely outcome for R2 and R3, and the report should say so in advance.

## Evidence base and its limits

The report rests on four research notes plus an earlier report on R2. Most primary sources were seen only as search summaries or abstracts, because many hosts failed to load, so claims below are marked **UNVERIFIED** where nothing more than a snippet supports them. Three conventions separate evidence from opinion. "Published" means a source states it. "Ours", "my inference" and "SIM (mine)" mark recipes, arithmetic and simulations written by the researchers, which are not validated in the microwave breast imaging literature. Project-internal results (the audit and the earlier tests on these same 504 scans) are labelled as such and are not literature.

The R2 conclusions can be stated briefly, since the earlier report covers them. No ground-truth-free per-scan reliability score has been validated against localization error in microwave breast imaging ([Reimer and Pistorius review, Sensors 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10255334/)); that review says current metrics need the tumour location and full knowledge of tissue properties, which makes them unsuitable for blind trials. The project's hand-made RRS-MBI score ranks inversely (AUROC 0.43, CI [0.36, 0.51]), and the best agreement-based candidates, sub-aperture and sub-band peak consistency, reach only 0.585 and 0.60 with lower bounds near 0.51 (project-internal). A simulation in the earlier notes (SIM (mine); 66 clusters, 80 positives, assumptions not estimated from the data) puts the minimum detectable AUROC for one pre-specified score at about **0.60 to 0.62**, and the 95th percentile of the best of six null scores at 0.584 to 0.592. Breast radius alone predicts R2 success with AUROC 0.69, falling to 0.53 within size strata, so any score that normalises by shell geometry is suspect. Predictions sit about 9 to 11 mm from the centre at every true distance, and the corrected pipeline does not beat "predict the centre" (30.9 versus 29.2 mm mean error). These facts matter here for two reasons: they set the statistical budget for R1 and R3, and they show that central tumours make the localization task easy for a trivial predictor, a confound that returns for size.

## Three notions, three labels, and why "within one class" is vacuous

### R1: clean of noise, clutter and drift

R1 has no direct ground truth, so the label must come from information independent of the detector's features. The notes propose two sources (my inference). The first is a natural-noise label: the residual energy of a scan after subtraction of its own adipose-only reference, judged against the same residual for pairs of adipose-only scans of the same shell design (divide the pair difference by the square root of 2 to get a per-scan sigma). A binary "clean" label is a threshold fixed in advance, for example the 75th percentile of the reference-pair distribution, and the continuous residual is analysed as well (Spearman), since a binary cut discards information. The second source is injected noise of known variance, which gives an exact label by construction and is the primary validation (section 5). Whether paired adipose-only references exist per shell design is unchecked; the project has 141 references, but pairing availability is unknown. R1 should be defined separately for three degradations that behave differently: random noise, drift (gain, phase, delay change between target and reference), and clutter or channel faults. A single "noise" scalar is not expected to cover all three.

### R2: localization success

R2 is the binary label LE at most 15 mm, with 84 positives of 504 (prevalence 0.167). Sensitivity analyses should use a radius-aware tolerance (radius plus 5 mm, mirroring the Gen-3 phantom work, which used radius plus 4 mm to 0.5 cm) and the continuous LE as a secondary target ([Reimer 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8704509/)). The trivial baseline is the array-centre prediction. Because central tumours are easy for that baseline, R2 scores must be evaluated after conditioning on true distance from the centre.

### R3: size correct, and the vacuous-tolerance trap

With radii of 5, 10 and 15 mm the class spacing is 5 mm, so a tolerance of 5 mm equals "within one class". A constant prediction of 10 mm is within 5 mm for **100 % of scans**, so that success definition cannot discriminate any model from a constant. The sensible label is the exact class, equivalently an absolute error under 2.5 mm (half the class spacing) when the estimate is continuous. The baselines follow from the class shares of 32/40/29 % (my arithmetic; the shares sum to 101 % from rounding). A constant "10 mm" predictor, which is both the majority class and the median, has accuracy about **0.40**, balanced accuracy 1/3, Cohen kappa 0, linear weighted kappa 0, mean absolute error about 3.0 mm (0.60 times 5 mm) and macro-averaged MAE (5 + 0 + 5)/3 = 3.3 mm. A constant "5 mm" predictor scores 0.32 accuracy and MAE about 4.9 mm. The bar to beat is therefore accuracy 0.40 and MAE about 3.0 mm, not zero error. Graded labels are secondary: ordinal error as the class difference in {0, 1, 2}, and the count of two-class errors (5 versus 15 mm), the only error with real consequence.

The tools for reporting come from the ordinal-classification literature, all seen at abstract level (**UNVERIFIED** in detail). Macro-averaged MAE, which computes MAE per true class and averages so that a majority-class guesser is not rewarded, is due to Baccianella, Esuli and Sebastiani and was used in SemEval 2016 and 2017 Task 4 ([SemEval task paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.01973); the primary PDF returned HTTP 403). Cardoso and Sousa argue that rank correlation loses information about absolute class values ([INESC TEC record](https://repositorio.inesctec.pt/items/7df8325e-7f56-4946-9ff6-ef6a6e91e17f/full)), and Amigo and colleagues propose a measurement-theory closeness measure ([arXiv 2006.01245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.01245)). Warrens reports that quadratically weighted kappa can behave paradoxically for tables with an odd number of categories, so linear weights are preferable for a single index ([Psychometrika 2012 abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/psychometrika/article/some-paradoxical-results-for-the-quadratically-weighted-kappa/BFBA88B72EF53081B4F11C26C2A4A50A)). For a continuous size estimate, Bland-Altman limits of agreement apply, but with a discrete three-level truth the plot shows three vertical bands, so a strip plot per true level is clearer, and the limits should be read against the 5 mm class spacing: limits wider than plus or minus 5 mm mean the classes cannot be separated. The standard error of a limit of agreement is roughly sqrt(3 s^2 / n) ([Bland FAQ](https://www-users.york.ac.uk/~mb55/meas/sizemeth)); at n = 504 that is about plus or minus 0.15 s per limit, and phantom clustering (about 7.6 scans each) pushes it toward 0.2 s (my arithmetic).

No published tolerance for tumour size estimates in microwave breast imaging exists. Clinical size-agreement figures (CT, MRI, ultrasound or mammography against pathology) show limits of agreement far wider than 5 mm, for example mammography against histology at -22.3 to +24.7 mm, but those summaries are **UNVERIFIED** and do not transfer ([BMC Cancer 13:328](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1186/1471-2407-13-328)). The RECIST convention of a 20 % change plus at least 5 mm shows how a tolerance combines a relative and an absolute part, but it is a progression rule, not a measurement-accuracy standard (**UNVERIFIED**, [PMC5737828](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/5737828)).

### Two layers for R3 and how the notions relate

R3 contains two things that must not be conflated. The first is a size estimator (image or signal features to a radius class). The second is a detector of when that estimate is right, a per-scan score whose label is "estimated class equals true class". The detector's label depends on the estimator, so the estimator must be frozen and trained under phantom-grouped cross-validation before the detector is evaluated. The three notions also overlap through shared causes. A tumour near the centre makes LE small and size error large simultaneously, and a larger breast leaves more room for the 15 mm tolerance. Report the 3 by 3 matrix of phi coefficients (binary labels) and Spearman correlations (continuous: noise residual, LE, absolute size error) with phantom-cluster bootstrap intervals, plus partial correlations controlling for true radius, depth and shell radius. Low correlation means one detector cannot serve all three and a single reliability score is a bad construct. For joint labels, a conjunctive R_all would have about 5 % prevalence or less (roughly 25 scans), too few to validate; the count of notions satisfied (0 to 3) or a 2-of-3 consensus is more testable, and at most one joint label should be pre-specified. No published framework for joint selective prediction across several reliability criteria was found (**UNVERIFIED absence**), so this multi-notion design is the researchers' synthesis.

## Detecting R1: noise, drift, clutter and channel faults

### What the literature supports

The one firmly sourced fact is that a vector network analyser (VNA) noise floor is set by IF bandwidth and averaging: each tenfold reduction in IF bandwidth lowers the floor by 10 dB, and averaging is more effective than bandwidth reduction for very low noise ([Keysight help, noise reduction](https://helpfiles.keysight.com/csg/e5080b/s2_opt/trce_noise.htm), read directly, vendor documentation). No measured per-scan SNR, noise floor, IF bandwidth or averaging setting for UM-BMID was found; the dataset descriptor (Reimer, Krenkevich, Pistorius, EuCAP 2020) and the repository README did not give them, and the repository page lists only empty-chamber reference subtraction as processing ([UM-BMID GitHub](https://github.com/UManitoba-BMS/UM-BMID)). Whether the data contain repeated identical scans is unknown. Gen-2 has 66 phantoms built from nine adipose shells ([IEEE DataPort](https://ieee-dataport.org/node/4383)), so shell and session are shared across scans.

Drift is documented as a real problem but not scored. A 2026 Politecnico di Torino paper proposes reflection-based calibration to correct temporal drift in a multistatic system (search summary only, **UNVERIFIED**, [Polito IRIS](https://iris.polito.it/retrieve/1332ab7b-5c02-40b9-a60b-72c61d10422a/Simplified_Reflection-Based_Calibration_for_a_Multistatic_Microwave_Imaging_System_Submitted.pdf)). A public four-antenna phantom dataset deliberately includes cross-day measurement, fresh calibration, antenna reattachment and oil replacement over 32 sessions, so it is a candidate external check for a drift score, though it reports no drift magnitudes ([IEEE DataPort](https://ieee-dataport.org/documents/four-antenna-s-parameter-measurements-3d-printed-breast-phantoms-machine-learning-tumor)). Rotational subtraction can degrade image quality when its angle is wrong and assumes an off-axis tumour (**UNVERIFIED** snippets, [Galway](https://research.universityofgalway.ie/en/publications/impact-of-rotational-artefact-removal-on-microwave-breast-images-2/)); the same assumption failure, a reference that differs from the target by more than the tumour, is the drift problem here.

For clutter rank, singular value decomposition (SVD) clutter removal is established. I-MUSIC discards the two dominant singular values to remove skin clutter ([Pagliari et al., BioCAS 2015](https://www.sld.cs.columbia.edu/pubs/pagliari_biocas15.pdf), search summary), and through-wall radar uses SVD to estimate and project out the wall subspace without a background scene, with target-to-clutter improvement close to background subtraction ([Tivive and Bouzerdoum, IEEE TGRS 2015](https://ro.uow.edu.au/eispapers/3043), summary). A 2025 radar paper states that SVD threshold selection "has not been discussed in detail in the literature" (**UNVERIFIED**, [MDPI Remote Sensing 17, 2697](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-17-02697/article_deploy/remotesensing-17-02697.pdf)). No paper was found that uses removed rank, leading-singular-value energy or spectrum slope as a scan-quality score. For channel faults, imaging arrays degrade gradually with receiver failures, with image RMSE under 10 % up to 25 % failed elements in one non-breast study (**UNVERIFIED**, [IEEE letter record](https://ersearch2.cvtisr.sk/vufind/SummonRecord/FETCH-LOGICAL-c293t-e091718b56089c23d4469bac8936fd11001a4919512d7cb245b543f76059629b3?lng=en)). That implies a count of flagged channels may predict success only weakly. The skin artifact also varies across channels in realistic scenarios, which breaks the assumption of many removal methods ([Galway repository](https://researchrepository.universityofgalway.ie/entities/publication/c31d84dc-95d1-4359-a0cc-f92eafb926b7)).

### Candidate detectors (all recipes are ours)

The recipes below were written from standard definitions and are not validated in the microwave breast literature. Let `S_t` be the target scan (1001 frequencies by 72 positions, complex), `S_r` its adipose reference and `D = S_t - S_r`.

| Rank | Detector (ours) | Computation | Direction for "clean" | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Second-difference noise floor, cross-checked by late-window power | Robust MAD of the second frequency difference divided by sqrt(6) (the exact variance gain of a (1, -2, 1) filter on white noise), per antenna; late-time median power after ICZT beyond the last physical return | Lower noise estimate, higher peak-to-noise SNR | Underestimates correlated noise; overestimates if the response has sharp resonances; late window also holds multipath, so it is an upper bound on noise |
| 2 | Pre-skin residual after complex-gain correction | Energy of `D` in the time window before the earliest possible echo, divided by skin-echo energy, after a per-antenna complex least-squares gain fitted on early time only; also the gain deviation, phase deviation and phase-slope delay shift | Lower residual | Gain fit can absorb tumour signal if fitted on the full band; delay slope mixes drift with real radius differences, so use same-shell references only |
| 3 | Rank of `D` above the noise edge | Singular values of `D` against a Marchenko-Pastur-type edge, `sigma * (sqrt(F) + sqrt(A))`, with sigma from detector 1 | Smaller rank above noise | Depends on an unbiased sigma; rank also reflects shell eccentricity and antenna position error |
| 4 | Channel-outlier fraction | Robust z (median and MAD) across the 72 positions of log power, noise estimate, late noise and skin delay, after removing the first harmonic fitted on `[1, cos, sin]`; flag |z| above 3.5 | Lower fraction | The 3.5 cutoff is the Iglewicz-Hoaglin convention, not validated here; imaging tolerates failed channels |
| 5 | Leading-singular-value energy of `S_t` | Energy fraction in the first two singular values, 95 % energy rank, tail slope | Descriptive only | Confounded by shell size and eccentricity; keep as a covariate, not a score |

The ranking weighs plausibility, independence from breast size and cost. Detectors 1 to 3 are the project's suggested pre-registered trio (late-window SNR, pre-signal drift residual after gain correction, rank of `D`), because six candidates have already been tested on these 504 scans and extra candidates multiply the multiplicity ([project-internal](#evidence-base-and-its-limits)). The two single-scan noise estimators fail differently (multipath versus smoothness violations), so reporting both and flagging disagreement is cheap. Their pre-specified direction is fixed before looking: higher noise, higher residual and higher rank mean less clean. Each estimate must be calibrated against repeat or reference-pair estimates, `sigma_rep = std(S_a - S_b) / sqrt(2)`, before use.

The core caveat for R1 is that a clean scan need not be an accurate one. Noise injection shows that a detector measures noise; it does not show that noise matters for localization or size. The dose-response test (error versus injected sigma on the same scans) is therefore a separate experiment. If LE does not degrade, noise is not the failure mode, and the project's own audit points that way: the localization failures traced to geometry, blob extraction and central pull, not to thermal noise.

## Detecting and estimating R3: what the physics allows and what the literature offers

### Published evidence on image-based size is almost empty

For radar-type DAS, DMAS or CF images, no paper reporting a size error in millimetres for any image-derived estimator (FWHM, threshold area, equivalent diameter, Otsu, edges) was found. The 2023 review uses full width at half maximum (FWHM) only as an image-quality metric and states that it "does not clearly describe the quality of the image": a homogeneous breast with a small fibroglandular inclusion can give a similar FWHM, and healthy and tumour-bearing breasts can give similar FWHM ([review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10255334/)). The only quantitative size-versus-truth results are clinical and for a different modality. The Wavelia first-in-human study fitted an ellipsoid to a persistent region of interest in 24 women (11 invasive cancers) and reported a correlation of r = 0.704 with histology against 0.65 for conventional imaging, which the authors called too small for meaningful statistics; individual errors ranged from 62.9 versus 65 mm to a twofold underestimate (26.6 versus 52 mm), and lesions under 10 mm were a stated limitation ([Moloney et al., PMC8631021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8631021/)). A signal-level empirical model for tumour diameter (1 to 35 mm tumours, 92.28 % "accuracy") exists but its metric is undefined in the abstract (**UNVERIFIED**, [Ulster record](https://pure.ulster.ac.uk/en/publications/determination-of-the-breast-cancer-tumor-diameter-using-a-uwb-mic/)). Adjacent fields warn that size is estimator-dependent: ultrasound sized areas depend on the iso-contour level (-2 to -6 dB), and GPR hyperbola fitting cannot reliably recover target radius jointly with velocity, with a 2 % velocity error giving up to 500 % radius error in one analysis (**UNVERIFIED** snippets, [PMC3051355](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3051355/table/T1), [Northumbria](https://researchportal.northumbria.ac.uk/en/publications/on-the-limitations-of-hyperbola-fitting-for-estimating-the-radius/)). Any claim that image size predicts tumour size on UM-BMID would be new and needs grouped validation.

Machine-learning claims are weak. A simulation of size classification from 1 to 11 GHz multichannel backscatter reports over 97 % accuracy for four classes of 0.5 to 2 cm at 10 dB SNR with PCA and local discriminant bases (Davis et al., IEEE TBME 2008; abstract level, **UNVERIFIED**, [record mirror](https://typeset.io/pdf/ultrawideband-microwave-breast-cancer-detection-a-detection-nhkf26o9dm.pdf)). A simulated three-antenna study at 8.5 to 11.5 GHz reports size RMSE 1.5 mm and R2 0.96 but used a random 80/10/10 split with no measured test ([PMC12349357](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12349357)). On measured UM-BMID, a snippet attributes 98.35 % "size classification" to a framework on MRI-derived signals, with no known split design (**UNVERIFIED**; Khalid et al. ISCAS 2023 exists per [dblp](https://dblp.uni-trier.de/rec/conf/iscas/KhalidHMARM0M23.html)). Since Gen-2 scans share shells and sessions across tumour sizes, a scan-level random split leaks shell identity, so such high accuracies are potentially artefacts (my inference, consistent with the review's warning that several machine-learning studies suffered leakage). No phantom-grouped size result with a majority baseline was found. No published measured amplitude-versus-radius curve for a 5, 10, 15 mm sweep was found either.

### Physical feasibility: 5, 10 and 15 mm radius at 1 to 8 GHz

The physics constrains what any R3 detector can do. All numbers in this section are my arithmetic unless a citation is given.

Size parameter. For a background of relative permittivity 5 to 10, the size parameter x = 2 pi a / lambda runs from about 0.23 (5 mm, 1 GHz, eps 5) to about 7.9 (15 mm, 8 GHz, eps 10). The Rayleigh regime (x below about 0.5) holds only below about 2 GHz for the 5 and 10 mm radii; at 4.5 GHz all three sizes are in the Mie or resonance region, and at 8 GHz the 15 mm tumour approaches optical scattering. The amplitude exponent versus radius is therefore not a single power. Rayleigh scattering gives cross-section proportional to a^6 (amplitude a^3), so 10 versus 5 mm is 8 times (18 dB) and 15 versus 10 mm is 3.4 times (10.5 dB). The reflection limit gives amplitude proportional to a, so 6 dB and 3.5 dB. The realistic band-integrated step is somewhere between: roughly **6 to 18 dB for 5 to 10 mm** and **3.5 to 10.5 dB for 10 to 15 mm**. Single-frequency amplitude oscillates with size (Mie ripples, and an analytic and simulation study found response grows with size "in an oscillatory manner", **UNVERIFIED** abstract, [ACES 2016](https://journals.riverpublishers.com/index.php/ACES/article/download/10219/8523/31901)), so band-integrated energy is the smoother cue. The Rayleigh formulas themselves are textbook ([Purdue ECE 604](https://engineering.purdue.edu/wcchew/ece604s19/Lecture%20Notes/Lect34.pdf), snippet). The Born approximation fails at the upper end (contrast about 4 to 9, internal phase shift up to about 18 rad), and tumour loss (roughly 8 dB per cm one-way, from remembered literature values, **UNVERIFIED**) makes back-face echoes unusable.

Resolution. Pulse range resolution is c / (2 B sqrt(eps)); for B = 7 GHz that is 10.7 mm at eps 4, 9.6 mm at eps 5, 7.1 mm at eps 9 and 6.8 mm at eps 10. Cross-range for a nearly full ring at about 4.5 GHz in eps 9 is about lambda/2, roughly 11 mm, and worse off-centre. Published experimental values are worse: a bed-based 2 to 9 GHz system had a modulation-transfer-function resolution of 12.4 mm (0.5 mm standard deviation in Results, written 5 in the conclusion) and a time-domain FWHM of 29.5 mm, with intensity falling about twofold over 7 cm of target position ([Reimer et al. 2025, IJMWT](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/CD816E28EAFDCD815FC1108BAA40AC62/core-reader)). A DAS FWHM of about 7 to 11 mm by 3 mm for a 7 mm target has also been measured ([PMC3035806](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3035806/)). Putting these together, a 5 mm radius tumour (10 mm diameter) is at or below one resolution cell, 10 mm radius (20 mm) about two, and only 15 mm radius (30 mm) clearly exceeds it. In a Gaussian model, FWHM is roughly sqrt(W_psf^2 + d^2), widths of 10 to 15 mm for 10 mm tumours against 20 to 30 mm for 30 mm tumours, so the sensitivity of width to diameter is smallest exactly where it is needed. CF narrows and nonlinearly compresses the peak region, which understates extent, and a delay-model error blurs and widens it, coupling size to localization quality; neither effect is quantified in any source found.

Confounds. Depth changes the two-way level by roughly 2 to 6 dB per cm (estimate from assumed adipose loss, **UNVERIFIED** inputs), comparable to the 10 to 15 mm step. Fibroglandular density shifts the tumour contrast factor by about 3 dB between eps_b 5 and 10 (Rayleigh contrast factor 0.72 against 0.51); density and heterogeneity lower SCR, SMR and maximum amplitude (the BRIGID phantom finding, tumour maximum spanning -54.9 to -24.5 dB, **UNVERIFIED** snippet, [Galway](https://research.universityofgalway.ie/en/publications/effects-of-interpatient-variance-on-microwave-breast-images-exper-2/)). A MAX/AVG metric even failed for a 4 cm carcinoma because large size lowered the ratio ([review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10255334/)), so SCR is not a monotone size proxy. Session drift of 1 to 3 dB and residual clutter of 2 to 5 dB add to a combined root-sum-square budget of 5 to 10 dB (my estimate). Against a 3.5 to 10.5 dB step that gives d' of roughly 0.5 to 1 for 10 versus 15 mm and 1 to 3 for 5 versus 10 mm. The plain conclusion is that a coarse small-versus-large call may be feasible, three-class exact estimation is marginal, and it is unlikely without depth and density normalisation. Matching each tumour scan to a tumour-free scan from the same session would remove most shell, density and session terms and leave depth and shape; it is the setting where a size-versus-energy relation has the best chance, and should be tested first as an upper bound. At deployment such a pair is unavailable, which is why the adipose reference matters.

### Ranked R3 detectors and estimators (ours, none validated)

| Rank | Feature | Pre-specified direction | Why it is plausible | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Band-integrated energy of the reference-subtracted signal in the tumour blob, depth-normalised | Higher energy, larger radius | Smoother than single-frequency amplitude; carries the 6 to 18 dB and 3.5 to 10.5 dB steps | Depth, density and session terms are of equal size; depends on location accuracy, which R2 says is poor |
| 2 | PSF-deconvolved width: `r = 0.5 * sqrt(W_meas^2 - W_psf^2)`, with W_psf from a point-target simulation or adipose scan | Wider, larger radius | Standard Gaussian-width subtraction (background knowledge, not cited); only 15 mm radius is clearly above the cell | PSF-limited for 5 and 10 mm; CF compresses width |
| 3 | Spectral tilt: slope of log amplitude against log frequency over about 2 to 4 GHz against 6 to 8 GHz | Flatter or earlier-rolling spectrum, larger radius | Rayleigh predicts slope of about +2, flattening as x grows with both f and a | Pure scaling prediction; no phantom source; resonances likely buried by noise and loss |
| 4 | Threshold-sensitivity curve of blob area (fraction of peak 0.3 to 0.7) | Flat curve, compact well-defined peak (a quality flag, not a size) | Separates compact peaks from broad clutter | No source; the shell ring is a compact, bright, wrong object |
| 5 | Grouped learned model on features 1 to 4 plus metadata | Fit inside grouped cross-validation | Wavelia handles density by repeating reconstruction over density assumptions ([PMC8631021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8631021/)) | 504 scans, 66 phantoms: at most a few fitted weights are supportable |

The detector of "size estimate correct" can reuse R1 and R2 signals (a clean scan, a confident location) as predictors, together with the estimator's own margin (distance of the estimate from the class boundary). None has any published support; each is a hypothesis. A fixed-direction rank mean of at most three or four terms has zero fitted parameters and therefore no optimism, and is the preferred combination.

## Validation protocol and the acceptance rule

### Data handling and baselines

Every split is phantom-grouped (ideally shell- and session-grouped), with repeated 5- or 6-fold grouped cross-validation and every data-driven step (standardisation, direction choice, selection, penalty, threshold) inside the loop. Intervals come from a phantom-cluster bootstrap in which any selection is redone in each replicate; cross-validation intervals are themselves optimistic ([Bates, Hastie, Tibshirani](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.00673v3)), so treat every interval as a lower bound on uncertainty. Naive AUROC intervals can be about 30 % too narrow at high intra-cluster correlation ([Ying et al., PMC8586066](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8586066)). Random cross-validation underestimates error under dependence ([Roberts et al. 2017](https://pred.uni-regensburg.de/id/eprint/1455/)).

Each endpoint has trivial baselines reported in the same table. For R1 they are a constant score and a metadata-only detector (antenna radius, session, shell radius, breast size). For R2 the baseline is predict-the-centre, and breast radius alone (AUROC 0.69, 0.53 within strata) is the confounder-only score. For R3 they are always-10-mm (accuracy 0.40, MAE about 3.0 mm, macro-MAE 3.3 mm), the median size, a metadata-only model (depth, shell radius, position) trained under the same grouped cross-validation (a high-capacity learner on metadata is the stronger baseline), and shuffled predictions. Add a raw scan-quality predictor (mean amplitude, raw SNR) as a baseline for R2 and R3 to show whether the reliability score is more than scan quality. A detector that does not beat the best metadata baseline carries no usable information beyond metadata. For R3 report exact-accuracy gain over 0.40, balanced accuracy, linear weighted kappa, macro-MAE and MAE (also as a skill score, 1 minus model MAE over constant MAE), the 3 by 3 confusion matrix and the count of two-class errors. Stratify by true radius for R1 and R2, and by depth and shell radius for R3; if the pooled AUROC exceeds every stratum, the pooled value is confounded (Simpson type), and the covariate-adjusted ROC fixes false-positive fractions within strata ([Janes and Pepe, Biometrika 2009](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:biomet:v:96:y:2009:i:2:p:371-382)). With about 84 R2 successes, three strata is the most the data will support.

### Synthetic noise-injection and recovery test for R1

The R1 detector is first validated on a known answer (protocol is mine, adapted from the standard noise-estimator practice of adding noise of known variance and checking bias and variance across levels, [arXiv 1904.02566](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.02566); the search found no canonical benchmark, **UNVERIFIED absence**). Take real scans, estimate natural sigma0 from reference pairs, and add circular complex Gaussian noise independent per frequency and antenna with sigma_inj over sigma0 on a grid of 0, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2 and 4, with at least 20 draws per scan and level. Three non-Gaussian degradations are injected and tested separately: a slow low-order drift in phase or amplitude across position, a scaled copy of another phantom's echo as clutter, and a random per-antenna timing jitter. Pre-specified recovery metrics are the slope and intercept of the estimate against sigma0^2 plus sigma_inj^2, Spearman against injected level, the minimum injected level at which the injected-versus-clean AUROC exceeds 0.8, bias at zero injection (the known overestimation at very low noise in image-noise estimators), and invariance to global gain, global phase rotation and small index shifts. Negative controls are references from a different shell, shuffled antenna index, a phantom-level label permutation, and a constant-feature detector (centre distance, antenna radius, session) that must not recover the injected level. Pass criteria are written before running, for example Spearman at least 0.9, zero-injection bias under 20 % of sigma0, nuisance-transform change under 5 %, and shuffled-reference AUROC at least 0.9. Real-data agreement with the reference-pair residual is a second, weaker check and is reported separately. If the label feature is also a predictor feature the AUROC is a tautology; protect against that by computing the label on a disjoint set of channels or frequencies, by using independent information (references, injected sigma), or by reporting the oracle AUROC of the detector on the label variable as an upper bound.

### Endpoints, multiplicity and the decision rule

Pre-specify, with a dated tag or public record before unblinding, three primary endpoints. E1 is the R1 detector's AUROC for the injected-noise label (and its dose-response to LE as an auxiliary). E2 is the R2 AUROC for LE at most 15 mm, after conditioning on true distance from the centre and against the centre baseline. E3 is the R3 exact-class accuracy gain over 0.40 (and the detector's AUROC for "estimate correct"). Add at most one joint label, such as the count of notions satisfied, as exploratory. Correct across the three with **Holm** (family size 3; Holm-adjusted intervals correspond roughly to 1 minus 0.05/K), and count every earlier score already tried on these scans (at least six, plus two composites) when interpreting a lower bound.

Set a minimum effect of interest in advance; the notes suggest AUROC 0.65 for a detector and +10 accuracy points for R3. Then apply one rule per endpoint:

| Decision | Condition |
|---|---|
| ACCEPT | Holm-adjusted p below 0.05 and the whole 95 % phantom-cluster CI above the pre-set minimum effect |
| REJECT | The whole 95 % CI below the minimum effect |
| INCONCLUSIVE | Anything else |

The rule's consequence is stark for this sample. The minimum detectable AUROC is about 0.60 to 0.62 for one score, with CI widths of 0.13 to 0.16 (SIM (mine), earlier notes); with Holm the half-width rises from about 0.08 to about 0.105. An ACCEPT at a 0.65 minimum would need a lower bound above 0.65, so a true AUROC around 0.75 or higher. The R3 arithmetic is the same: the standard error of accuracy at n = 504 is about 0.022, about 0.031 with an assumed design effect of 2, so 80 % power against 0.40 needs true accuracy of about 0.49 (my arithmetic), and an accuracy of 0.45 cannot be validated. Expect INCONCLUSIVE for R2 and R3 unless the effect is large, and treat REJECT as the informative outcome when the CI sits wholly below the bar. R1 is better powered because the label is synthetic and can be replicated many times.

The 504 scans have already served design (six candidate tests, two composites, the audit), so these endpoints are not fully confirmatory. Only phantoms never used before restore confirmatory status; Gen-3 is the natural external set but needs its own geometry handling and tolerance, and is my suggestion, untested. A test pyramid supports the code: unit tests on synthetic signals (a point scatterer at a known position gives zero error, scale-free scores ignore amplitude scaling), property tests (invariance to global phase and gain, ties giving AUROC 0.5, grouped splits never sharing a phantom across train and test, asserted), regression tests on frozen CSVs of AUROCs and baseline values, and permutation null tests (phantom-level label permutation centred at 0.5 with SD about 0.034 to 0.044, a random score returning 0.5, a planted signal recovered inside its CI). Metamorphic testing for scientific code without an oracle is described by [Kanewala and Bieman](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.01954.pdf), seen as a summary.

## Conclusion

The new reading is that the three notions fail for different reasons, so one reliability score is a poor construct. R1 is a measurement-quality question with standard tools and an exact synthetic answer key; it can be validated cleanly, but a clean scan does not imply a correct location or size, and the project's own audit shows geometry and blob extraction, not noise, dominated the errors. R2 is capped by power and a shared central bias that agreement metrics cannot see. R3 is capped by physics: a resolution cell of 7 to 11 mm, a depth confound of 2 to 6 dB per cm against a 3.5 to 10.5 dB size step, and a trivial predictor that already scores 0.40 make exact three-class estimation marginal, and coarse small-versus-large is the realistic ambition. Whether "5 versus 10 versus 15 mm radius" is distinguishable from reference-subtracted scans is an empirical question this project can answer cheaply, by testing band-integrated energy against true radius within depth and shell strata before building anything more.

For an internship report, the defensible contribution is the framing and the protocol, not a working detector. State the three definitions and labels, show that "within one class" collapses to a constant guess, present R1 detectors validated by injection recovery, pre-register the size features and directions, and report INCONCLUSIVE or REJECT honestly when the intervals demand it. Because the literature on image-based size estimation and on scan-quality scores tied to imaging success is thin to absent, a well-documented null or inconclusive result is itself a new, citable finding. Claims seen only as snippets, in particular the clinical agreement figures, the 98.35 % UM-BMID size classification, the 2026 Polito calibration paper and the 2025 IJMWT table values, must be read in the original before the report cites them.
