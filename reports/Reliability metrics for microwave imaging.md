# Replacing RRS-MBI: no validated score exists, so test agreement

No ground-truth-free per-scan reliability score has been validated against localization error in microwave breast imaging, on UM-BMID or on any other dataset. The published literature supplies oracle quality metrics (SCR, SMR, localization error) that need the tumour position, beamformer weights (CF, PCF, SCF, GCF) that act on pixels rather than scans, and one detection-theoretic framework (GLRT with a false-discovery-rate threshold) that has not been applied to this data. Neighbouring fields (ultrasound, SAR, GPR, seismic migration) offer richer formulas, but they validate them against image contrast or a global nuisance parameter, never against the position error of a picked peak. The project's own results say the same thing from the other side. The hand-made R ranks inversely (AUROC 0.43 [0.36, 0.51]), and the best agreement-based candidates reach only 0.585 to 0.60 with lower bounds near 0.5. A simulation in the notes shows that the best of six useless scores reaches about 0.58 to 0.59 by chance, so these values are close to the noise ceiling. The defensible replacement is therefore not one clever formula. It is a small, pre-specified set of agreement and null-normalised terms, evaluated within breast-size strata, with the trivial "predict the centre" predictor as the benchmark, and reported honestly as weak or null if it stays inside the noise band. Section 5 gives the ranked shortlist of eight candidates and the validation protocol.

## The literature defines oracle metrics and pixel weights, not scan-level confidence

The standard figures of merit all require knowing where the tumour is. Reimer, Solis-Nepote and Pistorius define SMR as 20 log10(Smax/Cmean) and SCR as 20 log10(Smax/Cmax), with Smax taken inside a disc of tumour radius plus 5 mm around the known position, and call a tumour "identifiable" only when SCR is above 0 dB ([Diagnostics 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7345629/)). The 2023 Reimer and Pistorius review tabulates eleven metrics (SCR appears in 22 of 76 quantitative papers) and states that current analyses rely on single-pixel intensity and full knowledge of true tissue properties, which makes them unsuitable for blind trials ([Sensors 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10255334/)). Only two entries in that table need no tumour location, the focal metrics f1 (peak divided by the product of FWHM area and the number of distinct areas inside it) and f (f1 times a boundary-distance factor), and they were built as fitness functions for permittivity estimation, not as confidence scores. The review's source for them (its ref. 98) was not retrieved, so their definition rests on the review's table alone.

The coherence family does not fill the gap. CF, PCF, SCF and GCF re-weight each pixel by channel agreement, and in microwave breast imaging they have been reported to misbehave. Elahi and colleagues found that CF-DAS suppressed clutter but often put its dominant response in a place inconsistent with the clinical reports, missed the lesion in 2 of 5 patients, and that coherence metrics "may reward responses from non-lesion regions" ([Sensors 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6022049/)). The exact PCF, SCF and GCF formulas used in the project notes come from background knowledge (Camacho 2009, Li and Li 2003), not from texts that were read, so they must be checked against the papers before coding. CF is also amplitude-independent: it measures channel agreement at the assumed delays, so a shell echo that is coherent at those delays scores high regardless of whether a tumour exists. Capon or MV outputs have not been used as a per-image confidence in any paper found; only beamformer comparisons exist.

The nearest approach to a ground-truth-free decision rule is statistical. Davis, Tandradinata, Hagness and Van Veen (2005) test each voxel against a clutter-whitened Gaussian null with a threshold set to constrain the image's false discovery rate, and report detecting and localizing tumours below 0.6 cm in numerical and experimental phantoms ([record](https://typeset.io/pdf/ultrawideband-microwave-breast-cancer-detection-a-detection-nhkf26o9dm.pdf)). This was seen at abstract level only (UNVERIFIED in detail) and was not tested on UM-BMID. The patient-image literature uses a MAX/AVG threshold set per dataset, which the review says depends on breast size, and the Gen-3 phantom work uses an SCR threshold of 1.5 dB plus a localization tolerance of tumour radius plus 4 mm ([Reimer 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8704509/)). The review's own recommendation, to compare healthy and tumour-containing reconstructions, is the closest published endorsement of what the project can do with its adipose-only references.

Published results on UM-BMID itself do not rescue the situation. The dataset is described in an EuCAP 2020 conference paper, not a journal article, according to the search record ([IEEE DataPort](https://ieee-dataport.org/node/4383)); no Scientific Data paper was found, so the premise that one exists is unconfirmed. Gen-1 and Gen-2 results in the literature are detect-versus-no-tumour classifiers (Gen-2 AUC 78 +/- 3 %, rising to 90 +/- 3 % only when the test set is matched in breast volume and tumour height), and the review warns that several of these suffered leakage or lacked a true test set ([review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10255334/)). Verified image-based DAS and DMAS numbers are Gen-3 only: with an adipose-only reference, DAS sensitivity was 19 % and specificity 44 %, and specificity on healthy phantoms fell below a random classifier because DAS and DMAS both produce a "hot-spot" artifact in healthy reconstructions ([Reimer 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8704509/)). That last point matters for this project: the hot-spot is shared by DAS and DMAS, so agreement between those two beamformers (candidate 6 below) is expected to confirm artifacts, not expose them. This is an inference from two published statements and is untested. No Gen-2 DAS localization-error table was found, so the project's 30 mm unconditional error cannot be placed against a published Gen-2 baseline. The only controlled localization numbers, 11 +/- 4 mm on 12 of 18 identifiable reconstructions, are conditional on SCR above 0 dB.

No paper reporting a trivial constant-location baseline, an inverted reliability score, or an AUROC for a ground-truth-free score was found in microwave breast imaging. The inverse ranking of R and the "predict the centre" comparison are therefore new methodological contributions, not replications, and should be labelled as such.

## Neighbouring fields supply formulas, but validate them against contrast, not position

Ultrasound has the best-characterised channel-coherence quantities. Lag-one coherence (LOC) is the normalised correlation between neighbouring channels at lag 1, averaged inside a kernel. For a coherent point target, LOC is approximately SNR/(1+SNR), so channel SNR is about LOC/(1-LOC). In vivo, LOC correlated with contrast (r = 0.74) and CNR (r = 0.66), but the derivation assumes uncorrelated noise and a triangular noise-free coherence, and variability grows below roughly -10 dB channel SNR ([Long, Bottenus, Trahey, PMC6378881](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6378881)); this is the only primary text in the neighbouring-field notes that was actually read in full alongside the Hurley-Rickard sparsity formulas. Short-lag spatial coherence (SLSC) gives better speckle SNR than B-mode but limited point-target conspicuity, which is a poor fit for a small tumour (snippet only, UNVERIFIED). Nobody validated LOC or SLSC against the positional error of a detected peak.

Autofocus metrics from SAR, sonar and GPR (entropy, sharpness as the sum of I^gamma, contrast as std/mean, kurtosis, Gini) are validated as objective functions for estimating a global nuisance parameter such as phase error or migration velocity. The evidence of failure is as strong as the evidence of use. An entropy criterion strongly underestimated effective permittivity in GPR imaging of tree trunks (mean relative error 50.3 % against 4.46 % for an SSIM criterion), because entropy favours over-focused images ([arXiv 2602.05308](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.05308), numbers from a search summary, UNVERIFIED). PGA beat entropy-minimising autofocus in low-contrast SAR scenes ([Sandia](https://sandia.gov/research/publications/details/analysis-of-sar-autofocus-performance-2012-10-01), summary). Moment-based wall-parameter autofocus has a non-unique solution ([SPIE 6547](https://photonicsforenergy.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/6547/654709/Performance-of-autofocusing-schemes-for-single-target-and-populated-scenes/10.1117/12.720085.full), abstract). Through-wall radar offers the closest conceptual precedent for CF as a self-consistency index: Burkholder and Browne report that propagation errors degrade CF, so it "can serve as a quantitative metric for autofocus" ([abstract record](https://search.isc.ac/inventory/49/1302103.htm), abstract only, UNVERIFIED). In this project's setting, entropy, Gini and kurtosis all reward the brightest compact object, which after reference subtraction is the focused shell ring. The project already found 60 % of blobs on the ring after the first fixes. These metrics stay in the shortlist only as nuisance-parameter autofocus curvature, not as peak-quality scores.

Seismic migration provides the most transferable idea, agreement across independent subsets of the data. Semblance is the normalised ratio of stacked energy to input energy, differential semblance and common-image-gather (CIG) flatness measure disagreement across offset or angle bins, and a published "seismic velocity confidence" attribute cross-correlates each gather trace with its neighbour to flag unreliable imaging ([SEG wiki](https://wiki.seg.org/wiki/Coherency_inversion), [earthdoc abstract](https://www.earthdoc.org/content/papers/10.3997/2214-4609.201412611)). The same sources carry the caveat that several velocity models can produce equally flat gathers ([TGS](https://tgs.com/hubfs/Technical%20Library/Technical%20Library%20Files/ep_bell_etal_june2017_modeluncertainty.pdf), summary), which is the seismic form of "agreement cannot see shared bias". No jackknife or bootstrap over channels was found in radar, GPR, ultrasound or seismic papers, so the sector-peak spread and leave-block-out jackknife below are recipes of ours, not published methods.

Two further families give reasoning but no validation. In geometry modelling, Lavoie, Okoniewski and Fear estimate effective permittivity from an image-quality fitness function and show that wrong permittivity moves and weakens the peaks without giving a millimetre sensitivity ([PLOS ONE 2016](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0160849)); skin-echo fits are used inside artifact-removal patents, and the fit residual would be a natural misfit score that the patents do not frame as one ([US 7,570,063](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7570063), summary). In selective prediction, the best published reference for how to judge a failure score is Jaeger et al. (below). The energy-distance, Mahalanobis, Wasserstein and SVD-spectrum scores in the signal-level note have solid general theory (energy distance is an MMD, [Sejdinovic et al.](https://pdf.arxiv.org/pdf/1207.6076)) but no microwave-breast application, no verified source for the 4-6 GHz sub-band being informative, and a built-in confound: a large target-minus-reference residual also arises from reference mismatch, drift or a different radius. Those scores measure "something differs", not "a tumour was localized", so only the cleanest variant (pre-skin residual energy) is shortlisted.

## Local evidence: weak agreement signal, and a size confound that survives stratification

The project's own tests on 504 scans from 66 phantoms (about 84 successes at an error of 15 mm or less) are the only evidence about this particular failure mode. They are project-internal, not literature, and they were run under heavy reuse of the same data.

| Score (project-internal) | AUROC for success | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Old R (D_norm, CF_norm, 1-CC, G) | 0.43 [0.36, 0.51] | inverted: D_norm and 1-CC reward the method's central pull |
| Sub-aperture half-split peak distance | 0.585 [0.51, 0.66] | lower distance = more reliable |
| Sub-band peak distance | 0.60 [0.52, 0.68] | lower distance = more reliable |
| Set A (pre-specified 4-metric rank mean) | 0.58 [0.49, 0.67] | 0.54 [0.44, 0.65] after stratifying by breast size |
| Set A, phantom-split held-out | 0.584 | |
| Train-selected top-3 composite, held-out | 0.648 | falls to 0.562 within size strata |
| Breast radius alone | 0.69 [0.57, 0.78] | 0.53 within size strata |

The composite result carries the key lesson. The three selected metrics (prediction distance to the edge, mean CF, prediction radius divided by breast radius) plus the shell-centre offset are breast-size proxies, and breast radius by itself predicts success with AUROC 0.69 while adding nothing within strata (0.53). A larger breast leaves more room for a 15 mm tolerance and a tumour that is farther from the shell, so any score that normalises by radius, or that depends on shell geometry, reproduces the size effect and looks better than it is. That is the project-level version of the confounding the notes warn about. In UM-BMID the antenna radius is fixed at 21 to 23 cm while shell radius varies across nine designs, so tumour-to-shell and tumour-to-antenna distance covary with size (my inference from the notes; no published source tests it). A matched-twin healthy-scan control, the review's recommended design, was not decisive here because healthy and tumour scans are a median 46 days apart in the metadata, which leaves session drift as an alternative explanation for any difference.

Statistical power is the second constraint. A simulation in the pitfalls note (SIM (mine); 66 clusters of about 7.6 scans, 80 positives, assumptions not estimated from the data) gives a null sampling SD of the AUROC of 0.034 to 0.044, a minimum detectable AUROC of **about 0.60 to 0.62** for one pre-specified score, and a 95th percentile of the best of six null candidates of **0.584 to 0.592**. Its cluster-bootstrap CI widths of 0.13 to 0.16 match the widths in the project log, so the log is plausible and not too narrow. The consequence is stark: set A at 0.58, the half-split at 0.585 and the sub-band at 0.60 sit at or just above the level that six useless candidates would reach by chance, and the two lower bounds (0.509 and 0.514) fall below 0.5 under Holm correction across six candidates, as the log already notes. The Holm-adjusted half-width grows from about 0.08 to about 0.105 (my arithmetic from the simulation SD). The consistency family should therefore be described as having a weak, unconfirmed signal that cannot be separated from chance with this sample, not as a validated score.

One further mechanism limits any agreement metric. Sub-aperture, sub-band and beamformer agreement estimate the variance part of the error and are blind to shared bias. The project's predictions stay about 9 to 11 mm from the centre at every true distance, the fixed pipeline does not beat a centre guess (30.9 mm against 29.2 mm), and adipose-only images peak at r/R below 0.25 in 62 to 65 % of scans. Literature that could confirm "central bias" as a property of DAS on a ring array was not found; the ring-symmetric aligned-residual mechanism in the notes (every bistatic delay at the centre equals about 2R/v, so a time-aligned residual focuses there) is an inference supported by Reimer's observation of central clutter in dense phantoms ([Diagnostics 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7345629/)) and by the project's adipose-only histogram, not a published result. A point-spread-function map from `sim.py` at several radii would settle it.

## Validating a score without leakage: phantom groups, a fixed predictor and honest uncertainty

Selective-prediction theory tells the project what to report and what the pitfalls are. Jaeger et al. find that rank-only AUROC ignores how a scoring function changes the underlying predictor, that average-precision-style scores are inflated by class imbalance, that calibration is a separate task, and they recommend AURC (area under the risk-coverage curve) as the primary metric ([ICLR 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15259); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.15259v2)). Because the localizer is fixed here and the score is only a side output, the "score changes the labels" pitfall does not apply, so AUROC for success remains legitimate. It should be reported next to a risk-coverage curve with a random line (flat at the overall failure rate), an oracle curve and a trivial-predictor curve, at pre-declared coverages such as 50, 30 and 20 %, with quoted effective phantom counts. At 20 % coverage there are about 100 scans and perhaps 5 to 15 effective phantoms, so those intervals will be wide. Ties need explicit handling: a feature that is exactly zero for 90 % of scans (the interleave term) makes AURC depend on tie-breaking, so use tie-corrected AUROC or average over random tie-breaks (my recommendation; the corresponding appendix in Jaeger et al. was not read). The foundational selective-classification papers ([Geifman and El-Yaniv 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08500), [Hendrycks and Gimpel 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02136)) supply the definitions. A simple baseline outperformed more elaborate confidence scores in the Jaeger benchmark, a reminder that engineered products such as R deserve suspicion; the failure of a product form (one badly behaved term decides the ranking; clipped terms create ties; a term that measures the method's bias inherits it) is our reasoning, not a published result.

Leakage control starts with grouping by phantom, and ideally by shell and session, because Gen-2 phantoms share adipose shells and reference scans. Roberts et al. show that random cross-validation underestimates error under dependence and recommend blocked schemes matched to the prediction target ([Ecography 2017](https://pred.uni-regensburg.de/id/eprint/1455/)). Cluster inference follows: Obuchowski's clustered ROC extends DeLong's method with design-effect corrections ([help page](https://www.lerner.ccf.org/quantitative-health/documents/clusteredROC_help.pdf)), and a head-to-head in eye data found naive AUC intervals up to about 30 % too narrow at high intra-cluster correlation, with the cluster bootstrap close to the clustered analytic interval ([Ying et al., PMC8586066](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8586066)). The consistency of the cluster bootstrap is established in [Field and Welsh 2007](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jorssb/v69y2007i3p369-390.html). Hanley-McNeil and DeLong were only seen as snippets (UNVERIFIED), and cross-validation intervals are themselves optimistic ([Bates, Hastie, Tibshirani](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.00673v3)), so every interval should be treated as a lower bound on uncertainty. For confounder adjustment, Janes and Pepe's covariate-adjusted ROC fixes the false-positive fraction within each stratum, and the pooled ROC can be too optimistic when case proportions differ across strata ([Biometrika 2009](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:biomet:v:96:y:2009:i:2:p:371-382)). With about 84 successes, three size strata is the most the data will support.

Conformal methods are useful for sizing regions but not for proving a score carries signal. Split conformal gives finite-sample marginal coverage under exchangeability, which holds only across phantoms here, so calibration must use whole phantoms and the effective sample is closer to 66 than to 504 ([Angelopoulos and Bates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07511); [Barber et al., limits of conditional coverage](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.04684v2); [beyond exchangeability](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.13415v5)). Conformal localization has been done for keypoints, producing circular or elliptical sets from heatmaps ([Yang and Pavone, CVPR 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.12246)), and a ground-truth-free reliability score can serve as a normaliser of the nonconformity score, so the radial conformal radius is r(x) = q_hat * s(x). The abstention rule "abstain when r(x) exceeds the tolerance" and the use of conformal risk control with loss 1{error > tol and score >= lambda} ([Angelopoulos et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02814)) are my readings of the abstracts, not published applications, and with roughly 84 successes any guarantee would be very loose. Treat conformal output as a sanity check of whether s(x) tracks realised error by decile, not as primary evidence.

For combining weak features, the notes support a hierarchy. A pre-specified rank or z-score average of at most three or four terms with directions fixed in advance has zero fitted parameters and therefore no optimism. A sign-constrained ridge logistic regression with an inner grouped-CV penalty is the second choice. Gradient boosting is not supportable at 80 events. Riley and colleagues' criteria replace the old 10-events-per-variable rule ([secondary summary](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12874-023-02008-1)), and the notes flag a to-do: run `pmsampsize` to count how many weights 84 events can support; the expectation that the answer is two or three is unverified. Each additional composite (rank mean, ridge, boosted) enlarges the multiplicity family, so the primary composite must be declared before outcomes are viewed. Calibration, if reported, should use a one- or two-parameter logistic recalibration with intercept and slope plus CIs ([Van Calster et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6912996)), not ECE. A decision-curve view of abstention ([Vickers and Elkin](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2577036)) is a transfer by us and is optional.

## Eight replacement candidates, ranked, and the protocol that should judge them

All candidates below are computable from the scan, its adi_ref_id reference and the reconstruction, need no ground truth, and are implementable with the project's ICZT, delay grid, DAS, CF map and reference subtraction. "Validated" means validated against localization error or success in the sense of this project. **None is validated in the literature.** Ranking weighs plausibility of independence from breast size and the central pull, local evidence, cost, and failure risk; it is a judgement, not a result, and it cannot be tested on the 504 scans without spending more of their statistical budget.

| # | Candidate | Direction (higher = more reliable) | Literature anchor | Validated vs localization error | Main unverified point |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reference-null percentile of peak statistic, conditioned on r/R and shell radius | exceedance over null (low empirical p) | GLRT/FDR ([Davis 2005](https://typeset.io/pdf/ultrawideband-microwave-breast-cancer-detection-a-detection-nhkf26o9dm.pdf)); healthy comparison urged by [review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10255334/) | No | null construction when reference is also subtracted; shell-radius conditioning is itself a size proxy |
| 2 | Sub-aperture sector peak spread (jackknife over position blocks) | smaller spread | seismic semblance and velocity-confidence attribute ([SEG wiki](https://wiki.seg.org/wiki/Coherency_inversion), [earthdoc](https://www.earthdoc.org/content/papers/10.3997/2214-4609.201412611)) | Locally: 0.585 [0.51, 0.66] | stratified AUROC for this term alone not reported |
| 3 | Sub-band peak spread (3-4 bands) | smaller spread | phase error scales with frequency (our reasoning) | Locally: 0.60 [0.52, 0.68] | band definition; multiplicity |
| 4 | Shell-echo model misfit (circle/ellipse fit of skin-echo delay vs angle: rms residual, centre offset, fitted eps_eff) plus optional autofocus curvature | smaller misfit | [Lavoie 2016](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0160849); autofocus precedent (abstract) | No; shell-centre offset was a size proxy locally | highest confounding risk; must be normalised and stratified |
| 5 | Pre-skin residual energy (drift / reference validity) | smaller ratio | none (ours) | No | whether session drift is the main failure source |
| 6 | DAS vs DMAS vs CF-DAS peak disagreement | smaller distance | none; hot-spot shared by DAS and DMAS ([Reimer 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8704509/)) | No | shared bias likely caps it |
| 7 | Lag-one coherence at the peak, z-scored against the null | higher channel SNR (LOC/(1-LOC)) after null z-score | [LOC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6378881) (validated against contrast, not position) | No | shell echo is coherent too; adipose null mandatory |
| 8 | Focal quality (f1/f) or peak-to-second-peak | higher, only after null normalisation | [review Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10255334/) | No | peak dominance rewards artifacts |

**Candidate 1, reference-null percentile.** Reconstruct the adipose-only reference scans (141 in the project data) with the same pipeline, and for each radial bin r/R (and ideally each shell-radius bin) estimate the distribution of the peak amplitude, the CF at the peak and the LOC. The score is the empirical exceedance of the scan's value at its predicted point, p = fraction of null values above the observed, or, for family-wise control, `np.mean(null_max >= obs_max)` using the image maximum over the breast mask. This is the empirical-null analogue of the GLRT/FDR threshold, and it directly targets the project's central finding that CF within 10 mm of the centre is about 3 times the breast mean on adipose-only scans. Its weakness is design, not formula: because each tumour scan is subtracted by its own adi_ref_id, the null images need adipose scans compared with a different adipose reference of the same shell, and whether enough such pairs exist is unchecked. The radial conditioning also risks importing size information.

**Candidates 2 and 3, agreement across data subsets.** Form K disjoint angular sectors (or odd/even positions, or contiguous halves) of the 72 positions, compute each sector's complex image I_k(x), and take the spread of the per-sector peak positions x*_k, optionally with the semblance SEMB(x*) = |sum_k I_k(x*)|^2 / (K sum_k |I_k(x*)|^2) and a leave-one-block-out jackknife variance ((B-1)/B) sum_b |x*_(b) - mean|^2 over B = 6 to 12 position blocks (the image is linear in channels, so each block removal is cheap). The sub-band version splits 1 to 8 GHz into three or four bands; a common geometry error moves peaks differently per band because phase error scales with frequency, but a shared bias such as the shell ring does not. These are the best-evidenced locally and the least expensive, which is why they rank 2 and 3, yet their stratified AUROCs have not been reported and the sub-band tolerance sets the multiplicity budget.

**Candidate 4, shell-echo model misfit.** From the simple-clean target scan (no reference subtraction), take the first strong arrival per antenna, fit the skin boundary centre and radius with `scipy.optimize.least_squares`, and use the rms residual, the fitted centre offset and the fitted permittivity relative to healthy scans of the same radius. It addresses exactly the audit's geometry failures (start angle, radius, a 13 to 15 mm shell-centre offset), and the logic follows the through-wall autofocus precedent, but locally the shell-centre offset was already a size proxy. For S21 the arrival is a transmit-leg plus receive-leg path with the ports offset by 60 degrees, so the monostatic circle model is only an approximation (note's own caveat). Use it as a flag only after residualising against size.

**Candidate 5, pre-skin residual energy.** Compute the fraction of target-minus-reference time-domain energy before the skin arrival, where nothing physical changed: `drift = (|D_t[t < t_skin - margin]|^2).sum() / (|S_t[gate_skin]|^2).sum()`. It is tumour-independent and flags reference mismatch, which the matched-twin control suggested could matter (46 days apart). It is entirely our construction.

**Candidates 6 to 8** are included because they are cheap and informative about failure. Beamformer disagreement (maximum pairwise distance among DAS, DMAS and CF-DAS peaks, or the Pearson correlation of normalised maps inside the breast disc) is expected to be weak because all three share the data, delays and shell bias. Lag-one coherence at the peak, `LOC = Re(sum_i s_i conj(s_{i+1})) / sqrt(sum |s_i|^2 sum |s_{i+1}|^2)` over neighbouring positions on the ring, is a standard definition (my write-up) that is only reliable after z-scoring against the adipose null. The focal metrics f1 and f, entropy, Gini and kurtosis reward concentration, so they stay last. Capon/MVDR is excluded: with a 13 to 15 mm geometry error the steering vector is wrong, and signal cancellation is the documented failure of adaptive beamformers ([Rindal et al. 2017](https://ntnuopen.ntnu.no/ntnu-xmlui/handle/11250/2486897), abstract).

**Recommended validation protocol.** First, freeze before looking at any outcome the primary score (a fixed-direction rank mean of candidates 1, 2 and 3 is the natural choice, because it has no fitted parameter), the primary endpoint (AUROC for error of 15 mm or less on all 504 scans, no post-hoc exclusions), the sign of each term, the band and sector definitions, the correction method and the threshold for "success". Second, split every fold by phantom, preferably by shell and session as well, using repeated 5- or 6-fold GroupKFold (for example 20 repeats) rather than leave-one-out, and place every data-driven step (standardisation, direction choice, feature selection, penalty, threshold) inside the loop; convert to within-fold ranks before pooling. Third, report AUROC with a phantom-level cluster bootstrap in which any selection is redone inside each replicate, state the number of phantoms with at least one success and one failure, and test the null with permutation at the phantom level (an approach the notes label UNVERIFIED as a cited method). Fourth, stratify: report AUROC within three breast-size strata (or the Janes-Pepe covariate-adjusted ROC), report the AUROC of breast radius alone as the confounder-only baseline, and accept a score only if its within-stratum AUROCs have intervals that exclude 0.5 and exceed the confounder-only baseline. Fifth, control multiplicity: treat the primary composite as one test and every other candidate as secondary under Holm (equivalent to 1 - 0.05/K intervals), and count every earlier score already tried on these scans (at least six, plus two composites) in K. Sixth, benchmark against the trivial predictor on the accepted subset, since composite A's gain came from accepting more central tumours where the pipeline equals a centre guess, and report the full risk-coverage curve with oracle and random lines. Seventh, add a negative control from healthy-minus-healthy pairs at the same and at different radius; the project's matched-twin attempt shows this is only decisive if the pairs are scanned in the same session, so check the metadata for same-day pairs first. Eighth, because the 504 scans have already been used for design, confirmation needs a set the design never touched. Gen-3, which has healthy and adipose references, different phantoms and a different frequency range, is the natural external set but needs its own geometry handling and a re-derived tolerance; that is my suggestion, not tested. Finally, state the minimum detectable AUROC (about 0.60 to 0.62 here) and the dependence of that figure on the simulation's assumed within-phantom correlation, which should be re-estimated from the data.

## Conclusion

The new finding is not a better formula but a better reading of the failures. The old R inverted because its terms (distance from the centre, central concentration) reproduce the pipeline's location prior, and the apparently improved train-selected composite (0.648) inverted again under size stratification because its terms were breast-size proxies, so the dominant risk for any replacement is confounding, not variance. The agreement-based candidates, the only family with local signal, are limited by a power ceiling: with about 84 successes and 66 phantoms, an AUROC near 0.60 cannot be told apart from the best of six null scores, and their ceiling is further capped by a shared central bias that agreement cannot see.

The practical recommendation for an internship report is therefore to present the replacement as a pre-specified, null-normalised agreement composite (candidates 1 to 3), validate it within size strata against the centre-guess baseline, and be ready to report the result as inconclusive. A well-documented negative finding (R ranks inversely; consistency terms carry only weak, unconfirmed signal; no ground-truth-free score has a published validation) is a defensible and original contribution on its own, and it is consistent with the review's call for artifact-aware, blinded-evaluation-ready analysis.
